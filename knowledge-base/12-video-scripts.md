# 12 — Video Scripts (Marketing & Sales)

Production-ready scripts for the Phoxta video programme. Written to the brand voice
in `05-go-to-market-and-brand.md`: **direct not aggressive · specific not vague
(numbers > adjectives) · smart not academic · warm not corporate.** No hype words.

**Pricing referenced here is the live pricing page** (`src/lib/plans.ts`): Starter
**$75/mo**, Growth **$250/mo**, Scale **$1,500/mo**; blueprints **$700–$5,000+**.
If pricing moves, this file moves with it.

> ⚠️ **Honesty guardrail — read before filming.** Phoxta is pre-launch. No script
> here claims customer counts, testimonials, revenue or "thousands of founders."
> Every proof point is either (a) something you can show on screen, or (b) an
> explicitly-labelled model. That is not a limitation — "content as proof" is
> pillar 1 of the GTM plan. The product demo *is* the social proof.

---

## 0. The spine

Every script below is a different angle on three sentences. If a video doesn't
serve one of them, cut it.

1. **Most people don't want to build a business. They want to run one.**
2. **Phoxta sells the working system — storefront, checkout, customers, AI agent — so your months go into the part only you can do.**
3. **It's yours: your domain, your customers, your data, your right to leave.**

**The do-not-say list:** revolutionary · unleash · game-changer · disrupt ·
passive income · effortless · "just" (as in "just click here") · any customer
count or testimonial until you have real ones.

*Landscape. YouTube, site hero, sales follow-up. Screen-led.*

## M1 · "Marketplace to live storefront"
**Length ~3 min · The core demo. Make this one first — everything else can reference it.**

**Cold open (0:00–0:15)** — Start on the finished storefront, live on a custom
domain, with a real order coming in.
> "This is a working business. It takes orders, it answers customers, and it's on
> its owner's domain. I'm going to build it from scratch in the next three minutes."

**Beat 1 — Choose (0:15–0:45)**
> "Every blueprint is a business that already runs — not a design. You can see what
> it does before you buy it. Fashion, car rental, restaurant, furniture, travel."
Show the live demo of one. Say the price out loud.

**Beat 2 — Provision (0:45–1:15)**
> "Buying it clones the whole system into your account: storefront, catalogue,
> customers, orders, the AI agent, the console. Not a copy of the files — a running
> instance with your name on it."

**Beat 3 — Make it yours (1:15–2:00)**
> "Now the part that's actually yours. Brand, palette, logo, copy, products, prices."
Change something visible; show it live on the storefront.
> "No code, no developer, no ticket."

**Beat 4 — Domain (2:00–2:20)**
> "Put it on your own domain. Link one you own or buy one here. Customers never see
> the word Phoxta — this is your business, not a profile on someone's platform."

**Beat 5 — The bit that matters (2:20–2:50)**
> "And this is the part a template can't give you."
Console tour: order → customer record → conversation → AI reply.
> "One place. Monday morning, you log in here."

**Close (2:50–3:00)**
> "From $700 once, and from $75 a month to run. Everything you just watched is on
> phoxta.com."

---

## M2 · "A Monday morning in the console"
**Length ~4 min · Goal: what ownership actually feels like · Persona: Elena, Priya**

Frame the whole video as a single Monday, 8:40 a.m., one coffee.

1. **Overnight (0:00–1:00)** — What happened while you slept. Orders that came in.
   Conversations the agent handled without you. A booking that moved itself.
   > "None of this needed me. That's the measure."
2. **Needs a human (1:00–2:00)** — The escalation queue. A refund request. A
   complaint. Show the agent handing over with the full thread attached.
   > "It escalates instead of guessing. That's deliberate."
3. **The decisions (2:00–3:00)** — The approval queue. The AI wants to change a
   price / send a campaign / fulfil an order. You approve or you don't.
   > "Every write it makes is either off, needs your approval, or automatic —
   > your choice, per action. And every one of them is logged."
4. **Close (3:00–4:00)** — Look at the clock: it's 9:05.
   > "That's the whole job. If your business needs more than this from you every
   > morning, something is wrong with the system, not with you."

## M3 · "How the AI actually answers"
**Length ~3 min · Goal: kill the 'it's just a chatbot' objection**

> The strongest possible version of this video is **you texting the business live
> on camera from your own phone.** Do that.

1. **The claim everyone makes (0:00–0:20)** — "Every company says they have AI now.
   Most of it is a chat bubble that reads your FAQ page."
2. **Prove it's connected (0:20–1:10)** — Text the business a real question about a
   real order. Show the answer. Then show the same order in the console.
   > "It didn't guess that. It read it."
3. **Every channel, one thread (1:10–1:50)** — Same customer on SMS then WhatsApp
   then email. One conversation in the Inbox.
   > "Your customer doesn't think in channels. Neither should your business."
4. **Where it stops (1:50–2:40)** — Be straight about the limits.
   > "It doesn't invent a refund policy you never gave it. It doesn't take an action
   > you didn't approve. When it's out of its depth it hands you the thread and says so."
   Show the governance toggles.
5. **Close (2:40–3:00)** — "An agent that can act is only safe if you can see and
   stop what it does. That's why the controls came first."

## L1 · "Why Phoxta exists"
**Length ~7 min · Founder-led. One take if you can. Face and voice, minimal graphics.**

This is the pillar-5 founder narrative. Structure it as three broken things —
straight from the vision doc — then the single insight that connects them.

- **Open (0:00–0:45)** — Don't open with the company. Open with the gap.
  > "The best ideas I've watched die didn't die because they were bad. They died in
  > the eighteen months between the idea and a system that could take money."
- **Broken thing 1 (0:45–2:00)** — The gap between concept and working system.
- **Broken thing 2 (2:00–3:15)** — The tools are tools, not businesses. Shopify,
  WordPress, Salesforce — all excellent, all leave you assembling. And you still
  have to design the AI layer yourself.
- **Broken thing 3 (3:15–4:30)** — The people who should work together — founders,
  operators, capital — meet too late and by accident.
- **The insight (4:30–5:45)** — Deliver this slowly; it's the whole company.
  > "If a business is mostly a configuration of one shared backend, then selling a
  > business is a configuration change. And a configuration change is the cheapest,
  > most repeatable thing software does."
- **What that makes possible (5:45–6:40)** — One business, built properly, sold to
  many owners, each one theirs. Marginal cost falls as the network grows.
- **Close (6:40–7:00)** — What you're asking them to do. Keep it small: go look.

---

## L2 · "Tear-down" — repeatable series format
**Length 8–10 min · One every two weeks (GTM pillar 2). This is the format, not one script.**

Fixed structure so it becomes a show:

1. **The business (0:00–1:00)** — What it is, who buys from it, what it charges.
2. **The revenue line (1:00–2:30)** — Build it on screen. Volume × price × frequency.
3. **The leaks (2:30–5:00)** — Where money actually escapes. Usually not price.
   *This is the segment people share.*
4. **What automation changes (5:00–7:00)** — Be specific and be honest about what it
   does **not** fix. Name at least one thing it can't help.
5. **The verdict (7:00–8:30)** — Would you buy this business? Say a real answer.
6. **Close (8:30–9:00)** — Where the blueprint is, what it costs.

**Standing rules for the series:**
- Every figure on screen labelled **modelled** or **actual**. Never blur the two.
- Always name one thing that doesn't work. Credibility comes from the caveat.
- Never end on a hard sell. Charter: *"We do not push. We illuminate."*

**Episode queue (each has a blog post to repurpose from):**
coffee subscription · salon · car rental fleet · travel experiences · restaurant ·
furniture · DTC apparel

# PART 4 — SALES VIDEOS
*Not for social. 1:1 async, deal-room, or follow-up. Lower production, higher specificity.*

---

## V1 · Personalised demo intro (60–90s)
Record per prospect. Same skeleton, three personal details.

> "[Name] — you mentioned [their exact situation]. Rather than send a deck, I
> recorded the actual thing.
>
> This is the [vertical] blueprint. Given what you said about [their constraint],
> the parts that matter to you are [X] and [Y] — I'll skip the rest.
>
> [45s of screen, on their two things only.]
>
> Price is [X] once and [Y] a month. If it's not right, tell me and I'll stop
> sending things. If it is, the next step is [one specific action]."

**Rules:** use their words, not your features. Never more than 90 seconds. One ask.

---

## V2 · Objection — "Isn't this just a template?"
**Length ~90s**

> "Fair. Here's the difference, and it's not marketing.
>
> A template is files. You still have to deploy it, connect payments, wire the
> emails, build somewhere for orders to land. The template is the first 5% of the job.
>
> What you get here is already running. There's a URL. It takes a real payment.
> There's a customer record, an order record, and an inbox — and an agent already
> answering on them.
>
> The test is simple: after you buy it, do you need a developer before you can take
> money? With a template, yes. Here, no.
>
> If you want to check that claim, buy the cheapest blueprint and try to break it.
> That's a better use of your time than another call with me."

---

## V3 · Objection — "What if Phoxta disappears?"
**Length ~2 min · The single most important sales video you will make. Do not soften it.**

> "Good question, and you should ask it of anyone you're about to depend on.
>
> Three things are true and they're written into how we operate.
>
> One — your customers are yours. Your customer list, your order history, your data.
> Not ours to rent back to you.
>
> Two — your domain is yours. It points where you tell it. Customers never see our
> name; there's nothing to untangle from your brand.
>
> Three — you can leave. That's a promise we made deliberately, because a business
> you can't leave isn't a business you own. It's a tenancy.
>
> I'm not going to tell you there's no platform risk. There is, with every provider
> you'll ever use. What I'll tell you is what you keep if it happens — and that
> answer is: the business."

---

## V4 · Investor group / operator (~3 min)
**Persona: Dan & friends, Priya, Tom**

1. **The problem with small deals** — diligence costs more than the deal is worth
   below $500k. That's why good small businesses go unbought.
2. **What changes here** — every blueprint is the same architecture. Diligence the
   platform once, not each business.
3. **What you can actually see** — the operating console, the unit economics, the
   real system, before committing.
4. **The ask** — one specific next step. Not "book a call."

# PART 5 — PRODUCTION NOTES

## What to put on screen
The product is the proof. **Screen-record more than you talk.** Highest-value
footage, in order:

1. The AI answering a real message with real order data behind it.
2. Provisioning → live storefront on a custom domain.
3. The approval queue — the AI asking permission, you deciding.
4. A brand change reflected live on the storefront.
5. The Inbox showing one customer across SMS, WhatsApp and email.

## Visual identity
Deep navy, gold, indigo, teal. Confident serif display, humanist sans body.
**Real workspaces, real screens — no stock-photo clichés** (the brand doc is
explicit about this). Generous whitespace. Motion only where it's purposeful.

## The CTA ladder
Don't ask for a call too early. In order of commitment:

1. See a live blueprint demo *(no signup)*
2. Read the tear-down
3. Browse the marketplace
4. Buy the cheapest blueprint and test it
5. Talk to a person

Short-form should almost always point at 1 or 2.

## Repurposing map
One tear-down → 1 long video + 2 short clips (the leak + the verdict) + 1 blog post
+ 3 LinkedIn text posts + 1 podcast segment. Film once, cut five times.

## Filming order (highest leverage first)
1. **M1** — the core demo. Everything else references it.
2. **S1, S2, S3** — three shorts, one afternoon, same setup.
3. **V3** — the objection video; it closes deals.
4. **L1** — founder story, when you can give it a proper take.
5. **L2** — start the tear-down series and hold the cadence.
