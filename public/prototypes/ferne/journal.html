Notes on skin, soil and slowness

Ingredient science, routines that survive real life, and mornings with the people who grow what we bottle.

Home / Journal /

Every batch we press starts with a phone call — the grower tells us the fruit is ready, and the clock starts. Vitamin A in rosehip degrades measurably within days of the seed being cracked, so the difference between an oil that works and one that merely smells nice is mostly logistics.

Why the timing matters

Cold-pressing at low temperature keeps the trans-retinoic acid precursors intact. We test each batch by HPLC before it's bottled; anything below our threshold is diverted to body products where the bar is different.

That's also why we don't hold stock for long. Bottles are numbered, and the number on the base of yours tells you the harvest week.

What to do with this

Use fresh oil, store it out of the light, and don't be precious — three drops morning and night is more effective than a heavy layer once a week.
