# The journey map — the book as a product spine

HBR's Entrepreneur's Handbook is deliberately linear: from "should I do this at all?" to "how do I
cash out?". That order is the spine of any "launch and grow" app. Each stage below names the
question it answers, the reference file to load, the frameworks in it, the **gate** (the verdict
a founder must reach before moving on), and the artefacts the stage produces.

The book itself warns the real journey is iterative; stages loop back (an experiment in stage 2
changes the model in stage 3, which changes the plan in stage 5).

## Two layers

Every stage now has **two** sources: the handbook chapter (the method, durable) and, where one
exists, a **2026 supplement** in `modern/` (what is actually true now, researched September 2026
with sourced numbers). Load the chapter for the questions to ask, the supplement for the current
answers, instruments, benchmarks and regional variations. See `modern/00-index.md`, which also
lists the seven places where the two layers genuinely disagree.

## Stage detail

### Stage 0 — Founder fit (chapter 1)
- Frameworks: Table 1-1 five trait clusters; the three must-haves; investor's three questions.
- Rules: preparation beats passion; decide under uncertainty; fail fast; listen to complaints.
- Artefacts: trait profile by cluster, gap list with mitigations (learn / co-founder / advisor).
- Feeds: the advisor's tone (low exposure → more hand-holding), the team section of the plan.

### Stage 1 — Opportunity (chapter 2, appendix B)
- Frameworks: Timmons' definition; 10 customer & market questions with confidence + test;
  Table 2-1 market evaluation grid; business-model canvas (9 blocks); lean startup loop
  (canvas → customer development → MVP); five characteristics of an opportunity; Figure 2-2
  risk-vs-return line; six economics questions; cost-structure table; Table 2-2 three-year pro
  forma; fit (3), durability (3), competition (6) question lists; the two final questions.
- Rules: define the problem before the solution; revenue lines are hypotheses until customers
  exist; reject anything below the risk-return line; compare against doing nothing; war-game the
  worst thing a competitor could do (e.g. -20% price).
- Artefacts: evaluated market grid, live canvas with hypothesis status, experiment log,
  opportunity scorecard, breakeven, draft pro forma, competitor war-game.
- Feeds: the opportunity and marketing sections of the plan (stage 4), the numbers test (stage 2).

### Stage 2 — Business model and strategy (chapter 3)
- Frameworks: the five questions (value / profit / differentiate / defend / be discovered);
  Magretta's narrative test + numbers test; Hamermesh & Marshall four groups (revenue sources,
  cost drivers, investment size, critical success factors); Table 3-1 twenty model analogies;
  hypothesis testing on the canvas; incubator vs accelerator table; Porter's three positions and
  five forces; six-step strategy formulation; network-effects box; Hoffman scale thresholds
  (10-100 → 100-1,000 employees, 100K-1M → 1-10M users, revenue > $10M); recognise → search → pivot.
- Rules: every model element is an assumption until tested; plan discovery (marketing) inside the
  model, not after; don't scale early; know what an incumbent or a funded copycat does to you.
- Artefacts: model summary (four groups + analogy), positioning statement, strategy document,
  discovery plan, scale-readiness flag.
- Feeds: company/offering/strategy section of the plan; the growth gate (stage 8).

### Stage 3 — Legal form (chapter 4)
- Frameworks: Table 4-1 six forms (sole proprietorship, general/limited partnership, C corp,
  S corp, LLC) with benefits and disadvantages; the full comparison (liability, tax, ownership,
  cost, fundraising fit, continuity); six partnership-agreement issues; S corp eligibility; limited
  partnership economics; sole-proprietor launch tips; 14-question form diagnostic.
- Rules: early losses or cash-distribution model → partnership/LLC; exit by sale/IPO or VC-bound →
  C corp now; plan the form's evolution before choosing. Non-US: map to local forms (UK sole
  trader / partnership / LLP / Ltd) and always say "check local law".
- Artefacts: recommended form with triggering rules, agreement term sheet for counsel, launch checklist.
- Feeds: the ownership sub-section of the plan; investor readiness (stage 7).

### Stage 4 — Plan and pitch (chapter 5)
- Frameworks: Figure 5-1 seven-section plan format with implied lengths; section-by-section
  schema (executive summary, opportunity, company/offering/strategy in four sub-parts, team,
  marketing, operating, financial); eight document formats compared; pitch-deck styles
  (presentation vs reading deck); Sahlman's 14 team questions; marketing ten-point checklist;
  ownership, IP, operating and model question lists; Tables 5-1/5-2/5-3; style rules; reader-lens
  self-check.
- Rules: people and model over numbers; the executive summary has two minutes to win; state the
  assumption behind every projection; tell readers how they get their money out.
- Artefacts: full plan, executive summary, 100-word and one-sentence pitches, two deck variants,
  projection tables with assumption log.
- Feeds: every funding conversation in stages 5, 7 and 9.

### Stage 5 — Startup-stage money (chapter 6, appendix A)
- Frameworks: three business types (Main Street ~70% / supply-chain ~17% / high-growth ~3%);
  business life cycle; source tiers (bootstrap → seed → reputation-driven); Figure 6-1 funding
  sources with percentages; Tables 6-1 to 6-3 opening balance sheet (size the need, then stack
  sources); Table 6-4 incubator / angel / accelerator / hybrid; McDuff eight-step launch sequence;
  crowdfunding and accelerator fit checks.
- Rules: compute the number first; stack sources cheapest-first; personal savings fund most
  startups; angels, PE and VC only follow a track record.
- Artefacts: launch capital figure, opening balance sheet, source stack with gap alert, family loan terms.
- Feeds: the financial plan; the loan-readiness scorecard (stage 7).

### Stage 6 — Launch and operate (the handbook's biggest gap, now covered by the 2026 layer)
The book supplies only the finance basics here: the three statements, how they link, the ratios
and breakeven. Everything about actually running the business comes from the 2026 layer.
- **Sell** (`modern/S01`): founder-led sales through the first 10-100 customers, discovery and
  qualification, the pricing conversation, pipeline math, outbound that survives the modern inbox,
  when to make the first sales hire, and expansion revenue.
- **Market** (`modern/S02`): positioning and narrative, growth loops, choosing one channel,
  content and search in the AI-answer era, community and creators, paid, brand versus performance.
- **Hire** (`modern/S03`): co-founder choice and equity splits, the first ten hires, scorecard
  hiring and structured interviews, compensation and options, global hiring, remote norms.
- **Run it** (`modern/S04`): the weekly operating cadence, the metrics review, SOPs, finance
  operations and the 13-week cash forecast, support operations, inventory and fulfilment,
  compliance, and what to automate.
- **Measure** (`modern/S05`): CAC, LTV, payback, retention and NRR, cohorts, burn and runway,
  and the metric set specific to SaaS, marketplaces, DTC, services or an AI product.
Artefacts: a working sales motion, one channel producing customers, monthly three statements, a
live metrics dashboard, and a weekly cadence someone other than the founder can run.

### Stage 7 — Growth-stage money (chapters 7 and 8)
- Frameworks: the banker's three questions (ability to repay, character, collateral) with four
  sub-questions; five lender ratios (current, acid-test, debt, debt-to-equity, times-interest-earned);
  Table 7-1 sources for growth (cash flow, debt, VC, IPO); instrument menu by stage; SBA loans box;
  matching principle; Table 7-2 eBay financing ladder; seed → Series A → Series B vocabulary;
  angels vs VCs comparison; three kinds of firms angels finance; Figure 8-1 flow of venture
  capital (2-3% fee, 15-25% carry, 1-in-15 bonanza); convertible preferred stock features;
  Balachandra's three presentation rules; Mullins' four downsides of VC; four ways to delay equity.
- Rules: short assets on short money, long assets on long money; stress-test with EBIT halved;
  angels fund 16x more companies than VCs and under 1% of US companies ever raise VC; line up VC
  6-8 months ahead; VCs want ~10x in 5-10 years; ask how the valuation was derived and get your own.
- Artefacts: loan-readiness score and document list, ratio traffic lights, funding route
  recommendation, dilution table, decoded term sheet, raise timeline, referral pipeline.
- Feeds: appendix C (valuation) whenever equity is priced.

### Stage 8 — Scale the company (chapters 10, 11, 12)
- Frameworks: three post-startup questions; blitzscaling rationale; growth-triggers table (which
  support functions must scale); four mechanisms for barring competitors; three routes into new
  markets; capacity by business model; outsourcing tips; four founding contributions strained by
  growth; Roberts' four leadership approaches and Table 11-1; sources of objective feedback;
  how to build a board (six rules); seven professional-management signals; founder's three
  options; three challenges to innovation; seven levers for the entrepreneurial spirit;
  creative-edge audit (seven questions); four reward types; eight attributes of an innovation
  process; Figure 12-1 innovation portfolio map; ambidextrous organisation.
- Rules: never outsource customer-facing links; never depend on one partner; move from managing
  content → behaviours → results → context; separate the future business from the core.
- Artefacts: growth gate verdict, capacity plan, leadership-mode transition plan, advisory-board
  charter, culture score, innovation portfolio chart.

### Stage 9 — Harvest (chapters 13 and 9, appendices C and D)
- Frameworks: why founders cash out (diversify / end of the line / begin anew); harvesting
  mechanisms table (IPO, M&A, ESOP, MBO/LBO, broker sale, shearing, family transfer); three M&A
  issues; asset vs stock sale; LBO candidate profile; ESOP five-step mechanics; valuation methods
  (book value, adjusted book, liquidation, replacement, P/E, EBIT and EBITDA multiples, DCF) with
  equity vs enterprise value; IPO candidate profile (~$100M revenue, deal ≥ $10M, 3 years audited);
  pros and cons of going public; eight IPO steps over 4-5 months; investment banker selection;
  private placement; Rule 144 holding periods and volume caps.
- Rules: sell when buyers are giddy; ESOP is the only mood-independent route; cash beats stock
  unless the stock is Microsoft-grade; value is a range, never one number.
- Artefacts: exit motivation, ranked mechanisms, multi-method valuation range, deal-term risk notes.

## What the handbook still does not cover
The 2026 layer closed the big gaps (sales, marketing, hiring, operations, metrics, AI, the
non-US world). What remains missing from both layers:
- **Product development and engineering management** beyond what `modern/S07` says about AI
  coding. No design process, no roadmapping, no technical architecture.
- **Industry specifics.** Neither layer teaches restaurants, clinics, construction or logistics.
  For Phoxta's five verticals use the `knowledge-base/` and the per-business research instead.
- **Personal finance and tax for the founder** as an individual.
- **Family succession and liquidation.** The book gives each one sentence; no supplement covers them.

## Reading the two layers honestly
- **The book is 2018 and US-centric.** Its statistics are 2014-2017. Where a supplement gives a
  current number, prefer the supplement and cite it. Where only the book has a number, say it is
  illustrative and dated.
- **The supplements are September 2026 and will age.** Quote a benchmark with its source number
  and year. `modern/S06`, `S09`, `S12` and `S07` move fastest.
- **Frameworks outlive numbers.** The book's method (define the problem before the solution,
  test the model's narrative and numbers, match asset life to financing term) is still sound.
  It is the instruments, thresholds and market conditions that changed.
- **Where the layers conflict, show both.** `modern/00-index.md` lists the seven live
  disagreements, founder mode versus delegation among them. Do not silently pick a side.
