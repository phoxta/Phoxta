# S02 — Marketing execution: positioning, channels, growth loops (2026 layer)
Supplements: `05-business-plan.md` (the marketing section and ten-point checklist are the book's
only treatment of marketing), `03-business-model-and-strategy.md` ("be discovered" is question 5
of the five, and Porter-style positioning is strategy-level, not message-level), and Stage 6 of
`00-journey-map.md`, which the book leaves empty. The handbook tells a founder to *describe* a
marketing plan for a reader; it never says how to find a first channel, write a position,
build a loop, or read a CAC. This file supplies that execution layer as of September 2026.

## What changed since 2018
- **Search stopped sending clicks.** 68.0% of US Google searches ended without a click in
  Jan-Apr 2026, up from 60.5% in 2024 and ~45% in 2016; AI Overviews appear on 20%+ of
  searches and cut click-through by roughly 60% where they show [1]. The "SEO → traffic →
  leads" plan in a 2018 business plan no longer describes reality.
- **Answer engines are a channel.** ChatGPT alone has ~800M weekly users and ~70% of AI-search
  usage [52]; LLM answers cite only 2-7 domains each [51]. Being cited is the new page one.
- **Paid acquisition got structurally dearer.** Meta CPMs are ~89% above 2020 levels [11];
  Shopify puts average US DTC customer acquisition cost at $226 in 2024, up 60% in five
  years [10]. Apple's 2021 App Tracking Transparency (initial opt-in ~16%) removed the cheap
  signal that made 2018-era Facebook ads work [43]; Google then reversed cookie deprecation in
  April 2025, so the web is now a permanent patchwork of consented and unconsented data [42].
- **Owned channels flipped from "nice to have" to the profit centre.** Email flows are 5.3% of
  sends but ~41% of email revenue; SMS flows 7.6% of sends but 45.2% of SMS revenue [28][29].
  In most of the Global South the owned channel is WhatsApp, not email (see Regional notes).
- **Creators replaced ad agencies for small brands.** The influencer industry reached ~$32.6B
  in 2025 [8]; 87% of brands expect to raise creator budgets and the money is going to nano and
  micro creators, not celebrities [7].
- **Social commerce collapsed the funnel in Asia.** TikTok Shop went from $4.4B (2022) to
  $45.6B (2025) GMV in Southeast Asia; Indonesia alone did $13.1B, +111% in a year [33].
- **The growth model replaced the funnel.** Reforge's "growth loops" (2018) reframed marketing
  from a linear pipe you keep paying to fill into compounding systems [13][14].
- **AI produces the content; distribution is the scarce thing.** 66% of marketers use AI
  (74% in the US) and only 4% let it write whole pieces [38]; Google's March 2024 "scaled
  content abuse" policy punishes volume without value regardless of who wrote it [40].
- **Budgets flatlined.** Large-company marketing budgets sit at 7.7% of revenue for the second
  year, half of CMOs at 6% or less, and 59% say it is not enough [5][6]. Founders compete with
  incumbents who are also cutting.

## Core ideas (current consensus)
- **Positioning is the input to everything else** (April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome*, 2019;
  *Sales Pitch*, 2023). The buyer's real question is "why you over the alternatives?", so
  positioning is defined relative to competitive alternatives, not in a vacuum [18][19].
- **Narrative beats features** (Andy Raskin, "The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen", 2016).
  Open with an undeniable shift in the world, not with your product; the strongest narratives
  are deliberately "wrong" — they trade completeness for memorability [16][17].
- **One channel carries the company** (Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares, *Traction*, 2015).
  Most companies get 70%+ of customers from a single channel at any stage; the job is to find it
  fast and cheaply, then go deep [15]. Peter Thiel's version: you won't have several equally
  good distribution strategies [15].
- **Loops, not funnels** (Brian Balfour with Casey Winters, Kevin Kwok, Andrew Chen, Reforge,
  2018). A funnel needs constant external input; a loop reinvests its output (users, content,
  revenue) as the next input, so growth compounds [13][14].
- **Earned before paid, and evolve the model** (Elena Verna, 2025). Paid channels buy access
  to someone else's audience; virality, word of mouth and UGC build your own. Every channel
  decays — expect to layer a new one roughly every 18 months and keep 20-25% of effort on
  exploration [20].
- **Penetration beats loyalty** (Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow*, 2010, Ehrenberg-Bass).
  Small brands suffer double jeopardy — fewer buyers who also buy less often — so growth comes
  from reaching all category buyers and being easy to notice and easy to buy, not from
  deepening loyalty among a few [25]. Evidence from decades of panel data; the startup caveat is
  that Sharp studied established categories.
- **Brand and activation are both required, in roughly 60/40** (Les Binet & Peter Field,
  *The Long and the Short of It*, IPA, 2013; 996 IPA cases 1980-2016). Activation pays fast and
  fades; brand pays slowly and compounds. WARC's 2024 "Multiplier Effect" work finds moving from
  performance-only to brand-plus-performance lifts ROI ~90%, and the reverse cuts it ~40% [12].
- **Zero-click is a strategy, not a defeat** (Amanda Natividad, SparkToro, 2022; Rand Fishkin
  & Natividad, *Zero Click Marketing*). Platforms reward content that keeps people on-platform;
  deliver the whole idea there and earn recall, then convert later [27].
- **Smallest viable audience** (Seth Godin, *This Is Marketing*, 2018). Pick the smallest
  market that can sustain you, serve it so well they spread it; "specificity is the way" [26].
- **Marketing is fuel × engine** (Emily Kramer, MKT1, 2025). Product marketing is the
  foundation, content and brand the fuel, growth the engine; avoid "random acts of marketing"
  copied from other companies [22].
- **Test before you launch** (Nik Sharma, 2021). Run cold-traffic ads to a one-page landing
  page with a real conversion event before you build inventory; a 2-6% click-through against a
  0.89% average tells you the message works [23].

## Decision rules & rules of thumb
- **If you cannot name the competitive alternative, you do not have positioning yet** — start
  with "what would they do if we didn't exist?" (Dunford [18]).
- **If the homepage opens with the product, rewrite it to open with the shift** (Raskin [16]).
- **Pre-launch: run the Bullseye once, test three channels for two weeks each with a fixed
  small budget, then put 70%+ into the winner** (Weinberg & Mares [15]; Kramer's 70/20/10 [22]).
- **If ad CTR on cold traffic is under ~1% and landing-page conversion under industry
  average, the message is wrong, not the channel** — fix positioning before scaling spend
  (Sharma: target 2-6% CTR vs 0.89% average [23]).
- **If a channel needs constant cash to keep moving, it is a funnel; find or build the loop
  that reinvests its output** (Reforge [13]).
- **Do not hire a growth lead before PMF, ~$1M ARR and self-serve revenue; do not hire one to
  reverse a decline** (Verna [20]).
- **Expect any working channel to decay; budget a new channel every ~18 months** (Verna [20]).
- **Never let brand fall below ~30% of spend, even at launch; new entrants should be nearer
  70:30 brand-heavy if they have any budget, mature brands 40:60** (Binet & Field practitioner
  ranges [12]). Founders with no media budget substitute founder-led content and community.
- **If SEO is in the plan, plan for citations, not clicks:** a cited brand keeps 35% more
  organic and 91% more paid CTR than an uncited one on the same query [3]; assume 60-70% of
  searches send no click at all [1].
- **Build flows before campaigns:** welcome, browse/cart abandonment, post-purchase and
  win-back flows return ~18x the revenue per recipient of blasts [28].
- **Prefer nano and micro creators, pay for content rights, and track with codes:** engagement
  is higher [8], budgets are moving there [7], and 45.9% of brands still track with promo
  codes because attribution is unsolved [7].
- **In a two-sided business, seed supply by hand first; do not spend on demand until the
  atomic network exists** (Rachitsky / Chen [54][55]).
- **CAC must be judged fully loaded** (platform CPA $38-58 vs fully loaded $212-230 on Meta
  [11]); use LTV:CAC ≥ 3 and payback ≤ 6-12 months as the gate [10].
- **Publishing AI content at scale without a value gate risks a manual action** — Google's
  March 2024 policy is method-neutral, and roughly 1,446 sites were hit in the first wave [41].
- **Marketing spend as a share of revenue is a lagging norm, not a target:** 7.7% is a
  large-company average [5]; early-stage software spends 10% on marketing plus 15% on sales
  [44], seed-stage 15-25% of ARR on marketing alone [46].

## Process / steps
1. **Position (1-2 weeks).** List competitive alternatives (including "do nothing" and
   spreadsheets), unique attributes, the value each enables, the customers who care most, and
   the category you will claim. Draft the one-sentence position and the Raskin shift statement.
2. **Prove the message with cold traffic (1-2 weeks, small budget).** One landing page, one
   conversion event (waitlist, pre-order, sample), 5-10 creative variants with the same message.
   Gate: CTR and conversion above channel averages [23].
3. **Choose the channel with Bullseye (2-4 weeks).** Rank the 19 channels, test the top three
   in parallel with equal budgets, pick the one that wins on CAC and quality of customer [15].
4. **Design the loop.** Write the input → action → output → reinvest cycle for the winning
   channel (e.g. customer → referral → customer; usage → content → search → user; revenue →
   ads → revenue) and build the product feature or content system that closes it [13][14].
5. **Build the owned base before you scale paid.** Email or WhatsApp capture on every page;
   welcome, abandonment, post-purchase and win-back flows live before the first big campaign
   [28][29].
6. **Launch as an event.** 4-6 weeks of waitlist building; Product Hunt or equivalent
   community launch; founder posts as zero-click content; creators seeded with product [35][27].
7. **Instrument.** Track blended and channel CAC (fully loaded), payback, LTV:CAC, branded
   search and direct traffic (brand proxies), AI-answer citations for your top prompts, and
   flow revenue share. Review monthly.
8. **Rebalance quarterly.** 70/20/10 across proven, emerging and experimental projects [22];
   add a channel when the winner's marginal CAC rises for two consecutive quarters [20].

## Worksheets, checklists & questions
Positioning canvas (Dunford):
- What would our best customers use if we didn't exist?
- What can we do that those alternatives cannot?
- What value does each of those attributes unlock, in the customer's words?
- Which customers care about that value most, and how would we recognise them?
- What market category makes our value obvious? What trend makes it urgent now?

Narrative check (Raskin):
- What undeniable change in the world do we open with? Who wins and who loses if they ignore
  it? What does the promised land look like? Which features are the "magic gifts"? What proof
  shows we have taken someone there?

Channel selection (Bullseye):
- Rank all 19 channels: inner ring (3), promising (6), long shot (10).
- For each inner-ring test: budget, duration, success metric, kill threshold, owner.
- After the tests: which channel delivered the best customers at the lowest fully loaded CAC?

Growth-loop worksheet (Reforge):
- Input → action → output. What is reinvested? What is the cycle time? What is the loop's
  conversion at each step? What would double it?

Owned-channel readiness:
- Capture on every entry page; welcome flow; abandonment flow; post-purchase flow; win-back
  flow; consent recorded per jurisdiction; WhatsApp Business profile and catalogue where relevant.

AI-search readiness:
- Ten real prompts a buyer would type; are we cited? Who is? Do we have an "about", named
  authors, structured lists, first-hand data, and schema on the pages that answer them? [51]

Budget sanity:
- Planned spend as % of revenue vs stage norms [44][46]; brand vs activation split [12];
  fully loaded CAC per channel [11]; payback ≤ 12 months [10].

## Regional notes
- **US.** CAN-SPAM is opt-out, so email is legally easy but deliverability-policed [47]. Paid
  social is the default first channel and the most inflated: Meta Q4 2025 CPM $22.98 [10]; ATT
  broke cross-app attribution in 2021 [43]. Product Hunt, Reddit and creator seeding are the
  standard launch stack.
- **UK.** PECR sits on top of GDPR: soft opt-in for existing customers, opt-in otherwise;
  fines to £500k under PECR and up to 4% of turnover under GDPR [47]. Binet & Field's IPA
  evidence base is UK-born and the 60/40 norm is most cited here [12].
- **EU.** GDPR opt-in consent (fines to €20M or 4% of global turnover) [47]; regulation also
  shows up in the data — a smaller share of EU search clicks flow to Google's own properties
  than in the US [2]. Expect cookie banners, consent-mode analytics and higher form friction.
- **Africa, Nigeria especially.** WhatsApp is the owned channel: Nigeria has ~51M users [30]
  and vendor compilations put adoption near 95% and marketing-message cost around $0.05 [31];
  small-business usage across Sub-Saharan Africa is reported near 78% (vendor-compiled,
  unaudited) [31]. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 requires consent for direct marketing;
  on 11 June 2026 the Federal High Court, Abuja ordered GTCO to stop unsolicited marketing to
  non-customers and to disclose the data source — possession of a number is not consent [48].
  Instagram plus WhatsApp catalogue plus bank-transfer checkout is the working DTC stack; card
  ad payments and Meta ad-account restrictions are recurring frictions.
- **India.** 535.8M WhatsApp users [30]; the DPDP Act requires explicit opt-in with penalties
  up to ₹250 crore [47]. Volume, low ARPU and vernacular content favour creator and WhatsApp
  channels over search.
- **Southeast Asia.** Social commerce is the store: TikTok Shop SEA GMV $45.6B in 2025, 71% of
  its global total; Indonesia $13.1B (+111%), Vietnam +150% [33]; social commerce was ~80% of
  Indonesian digital transactions in 2024 and ~60% of Indonesians buy via livestream [34].
  TikTok's paid ad reach has fallen 30-40% in several markets [58], so organic and shop-native
  affiliate content matter more than ad buys.
- **Middle East / GCC.** Digital ad market ~$13.6B in 2025, mobile-first, growing ~7% a year
  [49]; Arabic creator content and WhatsApp/Instagram commerce dominate SMB marketing.
- **Latin America.** Highest WhatsApp penetration anywhere (Brazil 93-99%, Mexico 93%);
  businesses spent $3.6B on the platform in 2024 and in-WhatsApp transactions grew 85% [32];
  digital ad spend ~$45B in 2025, growing ~11% a year [50]. Marketing messages cost $0.01-0.09
  each depending on country [32].
- **Everywhere:** "check local law" on consent, cooling-off rights, influencer disclosure and
  price-display rules before the first campaign.

## AI-era notes
- **Search behaviour moved first.** Zero-click reached 68% in early 2026, "the fastest
  acceleration in the last decade" [1]; even queries without an AI Overview lost ~46% organic
  CTR year on year as users click less everywhere [3]. The measurable job of content is now
  to be *cited* (by Google's AI Overviews and by ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity) and to be
  *remembered*, not to be clicked.
- **What being cited requires** (evidence from Seer, Profound and LLMrefs): first-hand data,
  named authors, direct answers in the first 200 words, structured lists (30-40% more AI
  visibility in one 10,000-query study [52]), fresh pages (citations drop after ~3 months [52]),
  schema and consistent entity facts across the web [51]. Only 2-7 domains are cited per answer
  [51], so this is a winner-takes-most contest — but the overlap with Google's top ten has
  fallen below 20% [52], which is an opening for new brands.
- **AI-referred traffic is small and converts well** — Vercel reports 10% of new sign-ups
  from ChatGPT referrals [52]; broader claims of 1% of all web traffic at higher conversion than
  organic circulate but are vendor-sourced (unverified).
- **Production is cheap; the moat is distribution and taste.** Two-thirds of marketers use AI
  and most rewrite what it drafts [38][39]; Klaviyo's AI product recommendations lift email
  clicks to 3.75% [28]; 93% of community professionals now use AI tools [36]. Ad platforms
  (Advantage+, Performance Max) automate targeting and creative assembly, so the founder's
  leverage sits in the offer, the creative concept and the first-party data fed in.
- **Hype vs evidence.** Programmatic AI content at scale is a documented penalty risk [40][41].
  "AI agents will run your marketing" is ahead of the data: HubSpot finds AI saving 1-2 hours a
  day, not replacing the function [38]. GEO tooling is a new vendor category whose benchmarks
  are mostly self-published; treat conversion claims as upper bounds. Attribution has got worse,
  not better, since ATT and the cookie reversal [42][43] — expect to rely on incrementality
  tests and brand proxies rather than platform dashboards.
- **What a founder should do now:** write the ten prompts a buyer would type and check who is
  cited; publish first-hand data the LLMs cannot get elsewhere; keep human editing on anything
  that carries the brand; use AI for research, variants and personalisation (Verna's warning:
  micro-optimisations rarely move the number [20]).

## Pitfalls
- Choosing a category before doing the positioning work; leading with features (Dunford [18]).
- Spreading a launch across six channels at once; never finding the one that carries 70% [15].
- Scaling paid before the owned flows exist, then discovering fully loaded CAC is 4-5x the
  platform's CPA [11][10].
- Reading platform-reported ROAS as truth after ATT and the cookie reversal [42][43].
- Cutting brand to zero because it is hard to measure; long-term effects decay silently [12].
- Planning "SEO traffic" as if it were 2018; two-thirds of searches send nothing [1].
- Publishing hundreds of AI pages without a value gate — a policy violation regardless of
  authorship [40][41].
- Hiring a growth lead to fix a retention problem, or rebranding to fix acquisition [20].
- Running a community you cannot connect to CRM or revenue — 83% cannot quantify it [36].
- Blasting purchased lists: unlawful under GDPR, PECR, DPDP and, since the 2026 GTCO ruling,
  enforceable in Nigeria [47][48].
- Treating a creator campaign as an ad buy: no content rights, no codes, no whitelisting, so
  nothing is learnt [7].

## Key terms
`positioning` — the deliberate definition of how a product is the best in the world at something a defined market cares about, set relative to competitive alternatives (Dunford).
`strategic narrative` — a company story that opens with a change in the world and casts the customer as the hero who must adapt (Raskin).
`category design` — creating and naming a new market category so the company that defines it becomes its default (Play Bigger).
`growth loop` — a closed system where the output of one cohort (users, content, revenue) becomes the input that acquires the next (Reforge).
`Bullseye` — the Traction method of ranking 19 channels, testing the inner three cheaply and focusing on the winner.
`zero-click search` — a query that ends with no click to any website; 68% of US Google searches in early 2026.
`AI Overview` — Google's generated answer block at the top of results; present on 20%+ of queries.
`GEO / AEO` — generative (answer) engine optimisation: earning citations in LLM answers rather than rankings.
`zero-click content` — platform-native content that delivers its full value without a link out (Natividad).
`mental / physical availability` — being thought of in buying situations and being easy to find and buy (Sharp).
`double jeopardy` — small brands have fewer buyers who are also slightly less loyal (Sharp).
`60/40` — Binet & Field's brand-building to activation budget heuristic.
`ESOV` — excess share of voice: share of category advertising minus share of market; predicts share growth.
`flow` — an automated, behaviour-triggered email/SMS/WhatsApp sequence (welcome, abandonment, post-purchase), as opposed to a one-off campaign.
`fully loaded CAC` — acquisition cost including creative, tooling, agency and people, not just platform spend.
`ATT` — Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt (2021) that collapsed cross-app ad attribution.
`atomic network` — the smallest self-sustaining group of users on one side of a network (Chen).
`smallest viable audience` — the narrowest market you can delight enough that it spreads (Godin).

## Build ideas for the app
- **Positioning Canvas** — alternatives, attributes, value, target, category, trend inputs → a
  one-sentence position, a homepage hero draft and a Raskin "shift" opener → every downstream
  asset starts from a tested position, not a slogan.
- **Bullseye Channel Picker** — business type, budget, audience location, product price →
  ranked 19-channel list with inner-ring test plans (budget, duration, kill metric) → founders
  stop spreading a launch across six channels.
- **Growth-Loop Designer** — chosen channel + product facts → loop diagram (input/action/
  output/reinvest) with step conversion fields and a "what closes the loop" feature list → turns
  marketing from spend into a compounding system.
- **Fully-Loaded CAC & Payback Calculator** — per-channel spend, tooling, people, creative,
  agency, orders, AOV, margin, repeat rate → blended and channel CAC, LTV:CAC, payback in months
  against the [10][11] benchmarks → the gate before any spend increase.
- **AI-Citation Checker** — ten buyer prompts → which brands ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity cite,
  and a page-level GEO checklist (author, first-hand data, lists, schema, freshness) → a
  practical replacement for a 2018 "SEO plan".
- **Flow Builder Readiness** — storefront/CRM state → which of welcome, abandonment,
  post-purchase, win-back and re-permission flows exist on email, SMS and WhatsApp, with the
  consent rule for the founder's country → owned revenue before paid scale.
- **Brand/Activation Budget Splitter** — stage, category, budget → recommended split (70:30 →
  40:60 by maturity) and the founder-led-content substitutes when budget is nil → keeps brand
  from silently going to zero.
- **Launch Runbook** — launch date → 6-week waitlist, creator-seeding and Product Hunt or
  regional-equivalent checklist with day-of hour-by-hour tasks → a repeatable event, not a hope.
