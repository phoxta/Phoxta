## What changed since 2018
- **Capital de-globalised, then re-concentrated on AI.** Global venture hit a record $510B in H1 2026
  [8], but ~50% of 2025 global VC went to AI [10] and Q1 2026 sent ~80% of all venture dollars there
  [9] — with ~88% of AI capital landing in US-based companies [8]. A non-US, non-AI founder competes
  for a shrinking slice of a record pot.
- **Tariffs are a first-order business variable again.** The US average effective tariff rate is ~11%,
  the highest since 1943, projected ~11.8% by end-2026 [16]; the IMF frames its 2026 outlook (growth
  3.0%) around trade-policy uncertainty [14][15].
- **The de minimis era ended.** The US suspended duty-free treatment for sub-$800 parcels from China
  (2 May 2025) and all countries (29 Aug 2025, EO 14324), permanent from 1 Jul 2027; the EU dropped
  its €150 threshold on 1 Jul 2026 [21][22]. Parcels under $800 into the US fell 54% [21].
- **Company formation is being re-papered.** The UK made identity verification mandatory at Companies
  House on 18 Nov 2025 and doubled the digital incorporation fee to £100 on 1 Feb 2026 [23][24]. The
  EU proposed "EU Inc" — an EU-wide 28th form, online in 48 hours for ≤€100, no minimum capital — on
  18 March 2026 [25][26][27].
- **State-run instant rails beat cards in the biggest emerging markets.** UPI did 23.2bn transactions
  worth ₹29.9tn in May 2026 alone [38]; Pix ran ~80bn transactions worth BRL 35tn in 2025 and is ~29%
  of Brazilian retail payments [39][40]. Neither existed in the handbook's world.

## Core ideas (current consensus)
- **Ecosystem rank is a supply map, not a verdict.** Startup Genome's 2026 ranking scores six success
  factors — Performance, Funding, Market Reach, Talent & Experience, AI-Native Cluster, R&D Engine —
  with Silicon Valley, New York and London unchanged at 1-2-3 [1][2]. It tells you where talent and
  capital sit, not whether your company can win.
- **Different indices measure different things.** StartupBlink ranks 110+ countries and 1,400 cities
  on quantity, quality and business environment across 40+ parameters [3][4]; Dealroom values
  ecosystems by the combined enterprise value of their companies (London $715B, 161 unicorns) [6].
  Always ask what is being counted before quoting a rank.
- **Domicile is now an explicit, contested decision.** UK Ltd, Delaware C corp, Nevada/Texas, Estonian
  OÜ and (possibly from 2027) EU Inc are genuinely different products on cost, investor familiarity
  and disclosure [23][27][32][34].
- **Diaspora is structural, not sentimental.** ~66% of US billion-dollar companies were founded or
  co-founded by immigrants or their children, and ~24% had a founder who arrived as an international
  student [54]; remittances to low- and middle-income countries run ~$690B, Nigeria $19.5B = 35% of
  sub-Saharan Africa [75].
- **The funding gap for women did not close.** Female-only teams took 2.3% of $289B of global VC in
  2024, up from 2.1% [51]; none of the 124 new US unicorns of 2025 had an all-female founding team
  [53], even as companies with *at least one* female founder took 27.7% of US deal value [52].

## Decision rules & rules of thumb
- **If your buyers and investors are US institutions, hold a US (or UK) holdco; otherwise don't pay
  for one.** Delaware is no longer automatic — ~62% of US IPO incorporations — but it is still the
  majority default, and Nevada/Texas carry their own governance trade-offs [32][33].
- **If you are UK-based, verify identity at Companies House before you need to file.** Existing
  directors must be verified by 18 Nov 2026 or the confirmation statement becomes a criminal-offence
  problem for the individual *and* the company [23].
- **If you are Nigerian and tech, get the NITDA Startup Label early**: CAC-registered LLC, under 10
  years old, ≥1/3 shareholding held by Nigerian founder(s), digital-innovation objects [28][29].
- **If your Nigerian company is near the small-company threshold, check *which* threshold** — the
  Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (₦50m) and the Tax Administration Act (₦100m) disagree, and professional
  services are excluded from the exemption entirely [30][31].
- **If you sell physical goods into the US, re-price before you re-market.** Every parcel now needs an
  HTS code, a formal entry and duty regardless of value [21][22]; model landed cost, not list price.
- **If you hold revenue in a soft currency, hedge by billing, not by trading.** The naira's forecast
  2026 range (₦1,350-1,520/$) is wide enough to erase a services margin in a quarter [20].
- **If you are hiring your first 1-5 people abroad, use an EOR**; incorporate when an entity costs
  less than ~$599/employee/month or a tax presence is triggered anyway [36][37].

## Process / steps
1. **Name the three geographies separately**: where the customers are, where the company is
   incorporated, where the people sit. They need not match; conflating them is the commonest error.
2. **Score the customer geography**: GSER factors for talent and capital [1], B-READY for operating
   friction [61], and the dominant local payment rail [38][39].
3. **Check the macro three**: policy rate, inflation and currency regime for both the revenue currency
   and the cost currency [14][17][19][20].
4. **Map trade exposure**: does anything cross a customs border? If so, price tariffs and the loss of
   de minimis into unit economics before pricing [16][21][22].
5. **Choose the domicile** against investor expectations, disclosure burden, formation cost and time
   (UK £100 digital [24]; Estonian OÜ via e-Residency [34]; Delaware/Nevada/Texas [32]; EU Inc later
   [27]), plus any local incentive forfeited by not incorporating locally [28][68].
6. **Register for the local incentive while you still qualify** — Nigeria's Startup Label [29],
   India's DPIIT recognition and 80-IAC holiday [68][69], UK SEIS/EIS advance assurance [59][60].
7. **Wire payments in the local order**: dominant instant rail → wallet/mobile money → cards; add a
   stablecoin corridor only for cross-border settlement, with a compliant on/off-ramp [39][41].
8. **Hire through an EOR first**, converting to an entity when entity cost < EOR cost or a permanent
   establishment is triggered [36][37].
9. **Build the funding stack region-first**: grants and sovereign programmes → debt or revenue-based
   finance → equity (in Africa, debt was 41% of all 2025 capital [11]), then re-test annually — every
   input above moved at least once in the last 18 months.

## Worksheets, checklists & questions
**Domicile questionnaire**
- Who writes the first institutional cheque, and what form do they require on a term sheet?
- What does incorporation cost and take — fees *and* verification time — in each candidate?
- What is the exit route from this form — trade sale, IPO venue, EOT, ESOP — and is it plausible here?
- If you flip later, what does that cost in tax and time?

**Country-entry checklist (per market)**
- Dominant payment rail, settlement time, and whether recurring billing is supported.
- Cost of accepting money and of getting it out (FX spread, capital controls, remittance cost).
- Employment: EOR available? Notice periods, 13th month, mandatory benefits?
- Tax: corporate rate, small-company exemptions, VAT/GST registration threshold, digital-services tax.
- Customs: is anything physical crossing a border, and at what duty?
- One named local counsel and one named local accountant before the first invoice.
- Quarterly review: policy rate, FX range versus your pricing indexation, tariff-schedule changes on
  any HS code you touch, and filing-rule changes in your domicile.

## Regional notes
Weighted for a London-based founder with Nigerian and US ties; every figure is a starting point for
local advice, never a substitute.

### United Kingdom
- **Formation.** A Ltd is cheap and fast, but the rules tightened: identity verification became
  mandatory at Companies House on 18 Nov 2025, existing directors and PSCs must verify by 18 Nov 2026,
  and the statutory registers moved to Companies House itself [23]; digital incorporation went from
  £50 to £100 on 1 Feb 2026 [24].
- **Funding.** The deepest market in Europe: $23.7B into UK startups in 2025, on a ~$34.2B pace for
  2026 (+45%), against $63.8B for all of Europe [5][7]. London carries $715B of combined enterprise
  value and 161 unicorns [6], ranks 3rd globally in GSER [1], and the UK was among the
  fastest-growing top-20 countries (+26.3%) [4].
- **Payments.** Cards plus Faster Payments and open-banking pay-by-bank; there is no dominant instant
  consumer rail of the Pix/UPI kind, so checkout stays card-led.
- **Hiring.** Protective employment law by US standards (notice, statutory sick pay, pension
  auto-enrolment); for a first overseas hire, EOR pricing (~$599/mo) is the benchmark to beat [37].
- **Tax & incentives.** SEIS/EIS are the distinctive advantage: 50% (SEIS) or 30% (EIS) income-tax
  relief for investors, and from 6 Apr 2026 EIS company limits rise to £10m/year and £24m lifetime
  with the gross-asset test doubled to £30m [60]. HMRC recorded SEIS at £276m (+14%) and EIS at
  £1,575m across 3,735 companies in 2024-25 [59].
- **Exit.** EOTs are a live succession route (~2,500 employee-owned businesses by mid-2025) but CGT
  relief fell from 100% to 50% on 26 Nov 2025 [57][58]; the UK is also the third-largest search-fund
  market, with 35 first-time funds [55].

### Nigeria and Africa
- **Formation.** Register the LLC with the CAC; for tech, add the NITDA Startup Label under the
  Startup Act 2022 — CAC-registered, under 10 years old, digital-innovation objects, and at least
  one-third of shares held by Nigerian founder(s) [28][29].
- **Funding.** Partech counted $4.1B into African tech in 2025 (+25%): equity $2.4B and debt $1.64B —
  41% of all capital, up from 17% in 2019 [11]. The narrower Africa: The Big Deal count was >$3B, +33%
  on 2024, fintech ≈40% ($1.37B) [13]. The Big Four — Kenya ($1.04B), South Africa ($715M), Egypt
  ($604M), Nigeria ($572M) — took >70% of the continent, and Nigeria was the only one to fall year on
  year [11][12]. Trackers disagree by hundreds of millions: quote the source, not "the number".
- **Payments.** Mobile money and agent networks, not cards: M-Pesa moved KES 41.68tn (~$322B) across
  46.41bn transactions in the year to March 2026 with 40.99m monthly actives [44], Kenyan
  mobile-money accounts passed 53m [43], Moniepoint processed ~14bn transactions worth ~$294B in
  Nigeria [46], and the CBN now ties every PoS agent to one principal institution [45].
- **Cross-border.** Stablecoins are a mainstream corridor: the IMF published on their Nigerian use in
  June 2026 [41] and Nigeria accounts for ~60% of sub-Saharan African stablecoin inflows [42] —
  against ~9% average cost to send $200 to the region conventionally [41].
- **Macro.** The CBN cut the MPR to 26.5% in February 2026 with inflation at 15.10% and falling for a
  tenth month [19]; the naira trades near ₦1,430/$ in a forecast 2026 range of ₦1,350-1,520 [20].
- **Hiring.** Deep, affordable senior engineering talent, but payroll, pensions and PAYE are manual;
  EOR coverage exists across the major markets [37].
- **Tax & incentives.** The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 took effect 1 Jan 2026: small companies are exempt
  from companies income tax, capital gains tax and the development levy — but the threshold differs
  between the Tax Act (₦50m) and the Tax Administration Act (₦100m), and professional services are
  excluded [30][31].
- **Diaspora.** Remittances are macro-scale — Nigeria received $19.5B, 35% of sub-Saharan Africa's
  total [75]. Diaspora customers, angels and credibility are an under-used distribution asset.

### United States
- **Formation.** Delaware remains the majority default but is contested: ~62% of US IPO
  incorporations in 2025, Nevada ~17%, Texas ~4%; of 18 companies proposing to leave, 13 chose
  Nevada, and SB 21 was Delaware's response [32][33].
- **Funding.** The deepest pool by far and increasingly AI-only: ~$510B globally in H1 2026 with ~88%
  of AI capital going to US companies [8][9]. Female-founded companies (≥1 female founder) took 27.7%
  of US deal value, a record $73.6B — yet none of 2025's 124 new unicorns had an all-female founding
  team [52][53].
- **Payments.** Card-dominant with ACH/RTP behind it; the US is the outlier among large markets in
  *not* having a consumer-default instant rail.
- **Hiring & tax.** At-will employment with state-by-state payroll and franchise-tax registration;
  non-US founders normally hire via EOR before opening state entities [36][37].
- **Trade.** The decisive change for anyone selling in: de minimis is gone (China May 2025, all
  countries Aug 2025, permanent July 2027) and the effective tariff rate is ~11% [16][21][22].
- **Immigration.** ~66% of US billion-dollar companies were founded or co-founded by immigrants or
  their children, and ~24% had a founder who came as an international student [54].

### European Union
- **Formation.** 27 national regimes today; "EU Inc", proposed 18 March 2026 (COM(2026) 321), would
  add a 28th — fully digital, online in 48 hours for ≤€100, no minimum capital, open to any limited
  company in a member state, with agreement sought by end-2026 [25][26][27]. Estonia is the practical
  route now: e-residents formed 5,556 companies in 2025, generating €125m of state revenue [34].
- **Funding.** $63.8B across Europe in 2025, on an ~$89.1B pace for 2026 [7], the UK ~37% of it [5].
  Climate tech, long a European strength, fell 19% in H1 2025 as AI absorbed capital [50].
- **Payments.** SEPA instant payments plus strong national methods; PSD2/SCA makes checkout friction
  a design constraint, not an afterthought.
- **Hiring & tax.** Employment protection and works councils make EOR-first entry the norm for the
  first 1-5 people [36][37]; R&D and innovation reliefs are national, so the incentive question is
  per-country, not EU-wide, and several member states run digital-nomad visas [35].
- **Notable.** The 28th-regime debate is about whether a startup can be "European" before it is
  national — watch it, but do not restructure until it is law [26][27].

### India
- **Formation & recognition.** Register the private limited company, then take DPIIT recognition:
  200,000+ (2.23 lakh) startups now hold it [68].
- **Funding.** State-led as much as private: a ₹10,000 crore Fund of Funds via SIDBI has deployed
  >₹9,000 crore through SEBI-registered AIFs [69].
- **Payments.** UPI is the market: 23.2bn transactions worth ₹29.9tn in May 2026, FY2025-26 volume up
  ~30%, 731 banks live [38]. Build UPI-first and treat cards as secondary.
- **Hiring.** Large, low-cost engineering supply; provident fund, gratuity and the new labour codes
  make payroll compliance the real cost — use an EOR or a local entity, not contractors [37].
- **Tax & incentives.** Section 80-IAC gives a three-year income-tax holiday within the first ten
  years subject to Inter-Ministerial Board approval, and angel tax no longer applies to shares issued
  on or after 1 April 2025 [68][69].
- **Notable.** India is the second-largest source of Claude.ai usage (5.8% of the total) yet low per
  capita [49] — a large absolute market inside a low-diffusion country.

### Southeast Asia
- **Funding.** The hardest regional market right now: $5.4B in 2025 with deal count at a six-year low
  and Singapore capturing ~91% of regional capital [66][67]; Indonesia collapsed from $6.9B at the
  2021 peak to $355.7M in 2025 [67]. Cheque sizes rose while cheque counts fell — a barbell.
- **Formation.** Singapore is the default holdco for regional operations, with local opcos elsewhere.
- **Payments.** Country-specific wallets and QR rails with growing cross-border interoperability;
  assume a per-country integration, not one regional rail.
- **Hiring & tax.** Singapore has the region's cleanest payroll and tax administration and is where
  most regional teams are employed; elsewhere EOR-first entry is the norm [36][37].
- **Notable.** Treat "SEA" as six markets, not one: capital concentrates in one city-state [66][67].

### Gulf / MENA
- **Funding.** The fastest-growing pool: $2.1B in H1 2025, +134% year on year, Saudi Arabia leading
  the year at $1.72B across 257 deals [62]. Sovereign funds — PIF, Mubadala, ADQ — are both capital
  and demand, because state digitisation mandates create procurement for local technology [63].
- **Formation.** Free zone versus mainland is the core decision (ownership, visas, trading rights);
  ADGM/DIFC-style zones are built for foreign-owned companies.
- **Payments.** High card and wallet penetration; cross-border ecommerce into the GCC grew ~41% year
  on year, so localised checkout and delivery are the constraint, not demand [71].
- **Hiring & tax.** Visa-linked employment and localisation quotas make headcount planning
  immigration planning; low personal income tax recruits, free-zone corporate tax is what to check [35].
- **Notable.** Capital comes with a presence expectation — an office, entity or hiring commitment [62].

### Latin America
- **Funding.** $4.1B across 681 rounds in 2025 (+13.8%): Brazil $2.1B and Mexico $1.1B (+53%) are
  78.5% of the region between them, and fintech alone took 61% [64][65].
- **Payments.** Pix is the reference case for a state rail displacing cards: ~80bn transactions
  (+25.7%) worth BRL 35tn in 2025, ~170m users (~80% of Brazilians), ~29% of retail payments, plus
  recurring billing via Pix Automático [39][40].
- **Formation & hiring.** Local entities are heavy and labour law protective, so EOR-first entry is
  common [36][37]; Brazil and Colombia run low-threshold nomad visas [35].
- **Tax.** Multi-layered indirect taxes (Brazil especially) mean gross-to-net pricing must be
  modelled per country before launch — a pricing project, not an accounting one.
- **Notable.** Cross-border ecommerce into the region grew ~34% year on year [71] — the constraint is
  localisation, not appetite.

## AI-era notes
- **AI adoption tracks income, not enthusiasm.** The Anthropic Economic Index finds Claude use per
  capita strongly correlated with GDP per capita (≈0.7% more usage per 1% higher income), with the
  gaps stable and no sign of low-use countries catching up; Singapore and Canada are among the most
  intensive users per capita while Indonesia, India and Nigeria lag [47][48]. Usage skews to
  augmentation (57%) over automation (43%) [47].
- **Within-country diffusion is fast; between-country diffusion is not.** Lower-usage US states are
  converging ~10x faster than historical technology diffusion [48] — do not generalise a fast domestic
  curve to a cross-border one.
- **Capital concentration is the sharpest AI-era global fact**: ~80% of Q1 2026 global venture went to
  AI [9] and ~88% of AI capital to US companies [8]. For a London or Lagos founder, "we're an AI
  company" is not a fundraising strategy; distribution and revenue are.
- **Where the hype outruns the evidence:** the claim that AI "flattens" the global playing field. The
  measured direction is the opposite — usage, capital and AI-native clusters (now an explicit GSER
  success factor [1]) are all concentrating. AI *has* genuinely cut the cost of operating across
  borders (translation, localisation, contract review, multi-jurisdiction research), which is an
  input-cost effect, not a demand one. Treat it as leverage on an existing advantage, and budget
  per-market legal review, since AI disclosure and human-review duties differ by jurisdiction.

## Pitfalls
- **Ranking tourism** — choosing a city because it moved up a table, without checking that the
  factor which moved (R&D, AI cluster) is the one you need.
- **Copying a US cap table into a country whose investors have never seen it.** SAFEs, US-specific tax
  reliefs and S-corp logic do not travel; local counsel first [30][31][59].
- **Assuming the DTC arbitrage still works.** The sub-$800 and sub-€150 duty-free windows are closed;
  models built on them in 2022-2024 are structurally unprofitable now [21][22].
- **Leaving a local incentive on the table** — Startup Label, DPIIT recognition and SEIS/EIS advance
  assurance are all age- or size-gated [29][59][68].
- **Treating an EOR as permanent** past the point an entity is cheaper, or assuming it removes
  permanent-establishment risk; and **restructuring on a proposal** — EU Inc is not yet law [25][27].
- **Believing AI erases distance** — diffusion and capital are both concentrating [8][47][48].
- **Reading one African funding number as *the* number** — trackers differ by hundreds of millions
  depending on whether debt, exits and undisclosed rounds are counted [11][12][13].

## Key terms
`GSER` — Startup Genome's Global Startup Ecosystem Report; the top-40 city ranking on six success factors.
`B-READY` — the World Bank's successor to Doing Business; scores economies on regulation, public services and operational efficiency.
`de minimis` — the customs threshold below which imports entered duty-free; suspended in the US (2025), removed in the EU (2026).
`28th regime / EU Inc` — a proposed EU-wide company form usable alongside the 27 national regimes.
`DExit` — US companies reincorporating out of Delaware, mainly to Nevada and Texas.
`Startup Label` — NITDA certification under Nigeria's Startup Act 2022 that unlocks incentives.
`DPIIT recognition` — India's startup registration that gates the 80-IAC tax holiday and other benefits.
`UPI` / `Pix` — India's and Brazil's dominant real-time retail payment rails.
`EOR` — employer of record: a third party that legally employs your staff where you have no entity.
`EOT` — UK employee ownership trust; a succession route with (now reduced) CGT relief.
`ETA / search fund` — entrepreneurship through acquisition: raise search capital, buy and run one SME.

## Build ideas for the app
- **Domicile Advisor** — inputs: investor type, customer geography, founder residence, exit intent →
  outputs: ranked jurisdictions with formation cost/time, disclosure burden, incentives gained or
  lost, and the cost of flipping later. Why: it is now the first structural question a founder faces.
- **Country Entry Pack** — inputs: target country + business type → outputs: payment-rail order,

  checklist. Why: turns a two-week research task into an afternoon.
- **Landed-Cost & Tariff Calculator** — inputs: HS code, origin, destination, unit price, shipping →
  outputs: duty, effective margin, post-de-minimis break-even price [16][21].
- **FX Exposure Monitor** — inputs: revenue and cost currencies, pricing terms → outputs: margin
  across the forecast range, an indexation recommendation, hedging-vs-billing comparison [20].
- **Incentive Finder** — inputs: country, company age, sector, turnover → outputs: eligible schemes
