# S11 — Leadership, scaling and culture (2026 layer)
Supplements: `11-leadership.md` (Roberts' content/behaviors/results/context modes, advisory boards,
"do you need professional management?"), `12-entrepreneurial-spirit.md` (culture, rewards, the
ambidextrous organization) and the organisational half of `10-sustaining-growth.md`. The book's
spine — the founder must move from doing to delegating, and step aside if unable — still holds;
what moved since 2018 is the *direction* of that advice (a loud counter-current says delegate less),
the evidence base (randomised trials on hybrid work and the four-day week), the shape of the org
chart (flattening, wider spans) and AI's arrival as a factor in span of control and headcount.
Hiring, equity, comp and interviewing live in `S03-hiring-and-people.md` and are not repeated here.
Load when a user asks how to lead a growing team, structure the org, set goals, run the operating
cadence, build a board, handle burnout, or decide whether to hire a CEO.

## What changed since 2018
- **"Founder mode" reopened the delegation question.** Paul Graham's essay (Sept 2024), written
  after a Brian Chesky talk at Y Combinator, argues the standard advice — "hire good people and
  give them room to do their jobs" — is manager-mode advice that leaves founders feeling "gaslit"
  [1]. Chesky himself says he never used the phrase and frames it as being in the details without
  micromanaging [2][3]. The book's chapter 11 prescribes almost exactly the trajectory Graham
  attacks; both readings are now live.
- **Org charts are flattening and spans are widening.** Amazon directed every senior leadership
  team to raise the ratio of individual contributors to managers by at least 15% by end of Q1 2025
  [6]; Gallup's 2025 analysis puts the average span at 12.1 direct reports [8]; Gartner's 2025 CEO
  survey frames AI explicitly as a delayering tool [9].
- **Remote work stopped being opinion and became evidence.** Bloom et al.'s randomised trial
  (Nature, June 2024, n=1,612) found hybrid cut quit rates about a third with no measurable
  performance cost [12][13]; a University of Pittsburgh study of 3M+ workers found RTO mandates
  raised time-to-fill 23% and cut hire rates 17%, attrition concentrated in senior, skilled and
  female employees [14]. The four-day week also got its largest trial — 141 organisations, ~2,900
  employees, six countries, six months: burnout down, satisfaction and mental health up [10][11].
- **Blitzscaling lost its default status.** Hoffman & Yeh's 2018 thesis — speed over efficiency
  under uncertainty [50][51] — was repriced after 2022 toward capital efficiency, runway and
  "default alive". (No formal recantation by Hoffman found: a shift in practice, not a revision.)
- **Founder mental health became a measured category.** Startup Snapshot's *Untold Toll* series
  puts founder anxiety at 37% and burnout at 36%, with startup *employees* worse on both [15].
- **Governance caught up with growth outside the US.** Africa's 2024-25 shutdown wave and India's
  BharatPe/Byju's/GoMechanic cases turned "founder-controlled, board-light" into a red flag
  [44][45][47][48].
- **AI entered the operating model, not just the product.** Shopify made "reflexive AI usage" a
  baseline expectation and required teams to show why AI cannot do the work before asking for
  headcount (April 2025) [26][27]; ICONIQ finds AI-revenue leaders run flatter than peers [25].
- **CEO turnover hit records.** 446 public-company CEO exits in 2025, the highest on record, and
  18% of incoming CEOs appointed on an interim basis versus 7% a year earlier [21][22].

## Core ideas (current consensus)
- **The founder's unique asset is context, not control** — Graham (2024): founder mode abandons the
  rule that a CEO engages only through direct reports; Jobs's annual retreat for Apple's "100 most
  important people" regardless of title is the archetype [1]. Graham concedes it is undescribed:
  "There are as far as I know no books specifically about founder mode" [1].
- **Founder mode is a licence that can be abused** — Chris Yeh (2024): in weak hands it becomes "a
  solipsistic system of governance where anything and anyone might be overruled"; his fix is
  *evidence mode* — the founder may overrule, but must do it on evidence and in the company's
  interest [4].
- **Depth is task-specific, not a personality** — the best reconciliation in the 2024-26 debate
  borrows Grove's *task-relevant maturity*: go deep where the team is new to the problem, back off
  where it is not [5] — Roberts' mode-mixing from chapter 11, restated at task level.
- **Companies need an operating system, written down** — Claire Hughes Johnson, *Scaling People*
  (Stripe Press, 2023): founding documents (mission, long-term goals, operating principles, team
  charters), operating cadences (business reviews, planning) and feedback/performance mechanisms;
  her four principles are build self-awareness, say the thing you think you cannot say, distinguish
  management from leadership, and come back to your operating system [34][55].
- **Most companies underperform on standards, not strategy** — Slootman, *Amp It Up* (2022): raise
  standards, increase urgency, narrow focus, align people; the enemy is tolerated mediocrity [35].
- **Written narrative beats slides for decisions** — Bryar & Carr, *Working Backwards* (2021):
  Amazon banned PowerPoint in 2004 for six-page narratives read silently for the first ~20 minutes
  of a meeting; the discipline is in the writing [38][39][52].
- **Match decision speed to reversibility** — Bezos (2015-16 shareholder letters): two-way-door
  decisions go fast, made by individuals or small groups; only one-way doors deserve heavyweight
  process. Big organisations apply heavyweight process to everything, which is what makes them
  slow [36][37].
- **Candour is a manager's job, respect is the precondition** — Kim Scott: *Radical Candor* (2017)
  = care personally + challenge directly (failure modes: obnoxious aggression, ruinous empathy);
  *Radical Respect* (2024) is its prequel — candour fails without respect for the person [40][41].
- **Transparency can substitute for hierarchy at the top** — Jensen Huang runs ~60 direct reports
  and no standing 1:1s, so that "there is no privileged access to information" [33]. It removes
  layers — and the private channel people use to raise problems.
- **Founder wellbeing is an organisational variable.** Startup Snapshot (Nov 2025) finds employees
  more burnt out than founders: a founder who cannot show vulnerability propagates that downward
  [15][17].
- **Flattening is not free.** Gallup finds no single optimal team size; what predicts engagement is
  manager quality, manager time on IC work (best under 40%) and weekly meaningful feedback — about
  seven in ten are engaged at *any* team size when the feedback is there [8].

**The OKR critique (2020s).** OKRs (Grove; Doerr, *Measure What Matters*, 2018) have no
peer-reviewed evidence base as implemented; the closest support is Locke & Latham's goal-setting
theory, which backs specific difficult goals *with regular feedback*, not this ritual [42].
Recurring failure modes: key results written as outputs ("launch feature X"); quarterly cycles that
freeze while the market moves; no data infrastructure to measure the KRs [42][43]. Treat OKRs as one
option among several (SMART, North Star + inputs, WhyGo) and pick on measurability [43].

## Decision rules & rules of thumb
- **If you are the bottleneck on *everything*, that is manager-mode failure; if you are the
  bottleneck on *nothing*, that is founder-mode failure** [1][4][5] — decide per decision.
- **If a decision is a two-way door, name one decider and give them a deadline.** Only one-way
  doors get a heavyweight process (Bezos) [36]. Keep a written list of which is which.
- **If you are about to delayer, check manager IC time first.** Gallup: keep managers under ~40% IC
  work and give weekly meaningful feedback; team size matters far less than either [8].
- **If you mandate full-time office, budget for a hiring penalty** — +23% time-to-fill, -17% hire
  rate, disproportionate loss of senior and skilled staff [14]. Hybrid is the evidence-backed
  default: about a third fewer quits, no performance cost [12].
- **If you want a wellbeing intervention with trial evidence, a four-day week beats most perks**
  [10]. Pilot six months with a control group and pre/post measures, as the trial did.
- **If more than one of the book's seven professional-management signals is true (chapter 11,
  p. 192), act now** — boards are increasingly reaching for interim CEOs (18% of appointments)
  rather than planning succession [21].
- **If the founder is the only person with full context, the company is one burnout away from a
  crisis** (37% anxiety, 36% burnout, half report stigma about getting help) [15] — a coach, a peer
  group and a written decision log are continuity infrastructure, not perks.
- **If your board has no independent director by Series B, fix it before you need it** (seed 2:1
  founders:investors, 2:2:1 from Series A/B) [49]; Africa's and India's 2024-25 failures are mostly
  board-absence failures [44][45][47].
- **If AI is your reason for a smaller team, write down which work it removes.** Shopify's test —
  "demonstrate why you cannot get this done using AI" — is answerable, and judgement, relationships
  and accountability are legitimate answers [26][27]. Key results still need a weekly data source.

## Process / steps
1. **Diagnose the mode per surface, not per company.** List your 10 largest recurring decisions.
   Mark each content / behaviors / results / context (chapter 11) and each one-way / two-way door
   [36]. Where the marks disagree with reality, that is your delegation backlog.
2. **Write the founding documents** — mission, 3-year goals, operating principles, team charters
   (Hughes Johnson) [34]. One page each. Undocumented culture does not survive doubling.
3. **Set the operating cadence.** Weekly leadership meeting with a written pre-read; monthly
   business review against `S05-metrics-and-unit-economics.md`; quarterly planning; annual
   strategy. Put the six-pager and silent-reading rule in the calendar invite [38][39].
4. **Publish the decision-rights map.** Per recurring decision: DACI or RAPID roles, reversibility
   class, and whether the founder may override (evidence mode: on what evidence?) [4][36].
5. **Choose a goal system and its measurement plan together.** If OKRs, write outcome key results
   and confirm each has a live data source before the quarter starts [42][43].
6. **Design spans deliberately.** Manager IC time under 40%, a weekly feedback ritual, and a layer
   count you can state out loud (AI-revenue leaders cluster at 4 or fewer) [8][25].
7. **Fix the work model with evidence.** Pick hybrid days, RTO or a four-day pilot; run six months
   with pre/post engagement, attrition and output measures [10][12].
8. **Measure the culture quarterly.** Engagement plus eNPS against the tech benchmark (median 17-18;
   72% engaged) [23][24]; act on two items and publish what you changed — 13% of tech employees say
   they see no change after surveys [23].
9. **Build the board and the founder's support before you need them.** Seed 2:1, independent by
   Series A/B [49]; advisory board per chapter 11 (p. 190-191); a coach, a peer CEO group and a
   written succession note [15][21]. If the professional-management checklist trips and you cannot
   adapt, move to chair and hire a CEO — with the handover defined in writing, or you produce what
   chapter 11 calls the illusion of letting go.

## Worksheets, checklists & questions
**Founder-mode audit (adapted from [1][4][5])**
- Which three areas will I stay deep in — permanently, by choice, and known to everyone?
- Which three have I delegated *with the authority to be wrong*?
- When I overrule someone, what evidence do I show, and where is it written?
- What is my skip-level mechanism (all-hands Q&A, "top 100" forum, customer calls), and how often?
- Has anyone told me in the last 90 days that I am too deep somewhere? If not, why not?

**Operating-system checklist (Hughes Johnson) [34]**
- Mission, 3-year goals, operating principles, team charters — written and shared?
- Weekly/monthly/quarterly/annual cadence on the calendar, with owners and pre-reads?
- One documented feedback mechanism and one documented performance mechanism?
- A planning process that allocates people and money at the same time?

**Decision hygiene**
- One-way or two-way door? [36] Who is the single Approver / Decider (DACI / RAPID)? What is the
  deadline, and what happens if it passes? Who disagreed and committed, and is that on the record?

**Org design review**
- How many layers between me and a junior contributor? (AI leaders: 4 or fewer) [25]
- What share of each manager's week is IC work? (target under 40%) [8]
- Which managers do not give weekly meaningful feedback, and if we delayer, who inherits the
  coaching they were doing? [8]

**Culture measurement**
- Engagement % favourable and eNPS versus the benchmark (tech median 17-18) [23]
- The two lowest-scoring items — and the one change we will ship this quarter
- Tech-sector chronic gaps: growth/progression, social connection, manager support [23]
- Do people believe surveys change anything? (13% say no in tech) [23]

**Founder wellbeing**
- Hours slept, days fully off in the last month, and who else can run the weekly meeting
- Do I have a coach, a peer group, or neither? (77% of founders get no professional help) [16]
- Who on the board knows how I am actually doing? If I were unavailable for 30 days, what breaks,
  and is that written down?

## Regional notes
- **US.** The flattening story is loudest here: Amazon's IC:manager directive and five-day RTO
  [6][7], Meta and others cutting layers [54], record public-company CEO exits and a jump in interim
  appointments [21][22]. Founder-friendly governance (dual-class, founder-controlled boards) is
  still available but drew more scrutiny after 2022. At-will employment makes delayering fast and
  legally cheap relative to Europe.
- **UK and EU.** Delayering is slower and costlier: collective-consultation thresholds, works
  councils in Germany, the Netherlands and France, statutory notice — a "15% ratio" memo is not
  executable the same way. The UK ran the largest four-day-week pilots and supplied part of the 2025
  trial dataset [10][11]. The EU AI Act's high-risk classification for employment-related AI (see
  `S03-hiring-and-people.md`) also covers AI used in promotion, task allocation and monitoring —
  relevant the moment you use AI to manage people.
- **Africa (Nigeria especially).** 2024-25 was a governance reckoning: the 2024 Startup Graveyard
  analysis attributes 58% of failures to financial difficulty, 27% to operational issues and 17% to
  regulatory ones, with weak governance as the connective tissue [44]; Launch Base Africa tracked
  30+ high-profile crises in 2025, Nigeria the largest single share [45]; Thepeer's public
  post-mortem put roughly $1.2M unaccounted for [46]. For a Lagos-based founder: appoint a
  non-executive director before Series A, separate founder and company bank accounts formally, keep
  board minutes, and expect governance diligence as heavy as traction diligence. Dual-currency pay,
  power and connectivity costs also make "office days" a different calculation than in the OECD.
- **India.** BharatPe, Byju's, GoMechanic and BluSmart made governance the dominant investor theme;
  the structural gap is that listed-company rules (SEBI LODR) are strict while unlisted startups are
  largely unregulated [47][48]. CII has published a voluntary governance charter for startups [47].
  Expect audit-committee, related-party and revenue-recognition questions early.
- **Southeast Asia, the Middle East and LatAm.** Family- and conglomerate-linked capital makes board
  seats relational rather than purely financial; plan for a chair who is also an investor-operator.
  Cross-time-zone and cross-border teams (normal at seed stage across Argentina, Brazil, Colombia
  and Mexico) make written asynchronous cadences — six-pagers, decision logs — more valuable than
  the US default of synchronous meetings, and make the hybrid evidence [12] matter more than the
  RTO debate.

## AI-era notes
- **AI is now an argument in org design, not just tooling.** Gartner's 2025 CEO survey frames AI as
  a delayering opportunity [9]; ICONIQ finds companies earning most revenue from AI run flatter —
  72% at four or fewer layers versus 56% of peers [25]. Whether flat *causes* fast, or fast
  companies simply end up flat, is not established by that survey.
- **Headcount gates moved.** Shopify's April 2025 memo made "reflexive AI usage" a baseline
  expectation, added AI usage to performance and peer reviews, and required teams to demonstrate
  why AI cannot do the work before requesting headcount [26][27][28]. Copying the rule without the
  enablement (tools, training, worked examples) produces theatre.
- **A new managerial skill: supervising agents.** Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index (31,000
  professionals, 31 countries) names the "agent boss" role and the human-agent ratio as a planning
  question; 46% of leaders said their org is fully automating some workflows with agents [29][30].
- **The evidence is thinner than the rhetoric.** MIT's NANDA study found 95% of enterprise GenAI
  pilots delivered no measurable P&L impact [32]; Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to
  be cancelled by end-2027 on cost, unclear value or weak risk controls, and estimates only ~130 of
  thousands of self-described agentic vendors are real [31]. ICONIQ's own data shows agent
  deployments averaging under 30% productivity impact across revenue bands [25].
- **Where AI does show up in leadership work today:** drafting and critiquing six-pagers and
  PR-FAQs; summarising customer calls and support threads so the founder keeps context at scale
  (the thing founder mode is really about); synthesising engagement-survey free text; preparing
  board packs; rehearsing hard conversations. None of these need an agent framework.
- **Risks specific to leaders.** (1) Widening spans with AI without adding feedback capacity —
  feedback, not headcount arithmetic, drives engagement [8]. (2) Letting AI write the values and
  the review text, producing culture documents nobody owns [23]. (3) AI-assisted monitoring crossing
  into EU AI Act high-risk territory. (4) Banking an AI-productivity claim you have not measured —
  MIT and Gartner both say most do not survive contact with the P&L [31][32].
- **A defensible 2026 stance:** use AI to remove the reporting and summarising work that made middle
  management expensive, keep coaching and feedback human, and measure the substitution before you
  put it in a plan.

## Pitfalls
- **Using "founder mode" as cover for micromanagement.** Graham predicted it himself: once named, it
  becomes an excuse [1]; Yeh calls the failure state autocracy without evidence [4].
- **Copying a practice without its preconditions.** Huang's no-1:1s works with radical information
  symmetry and a specific kind of company [33]; Netflix-style candour works with Netflix-level
  severance; Amazon's six-pagers work because writing is a promotion criterion.
- **Delayering to a target number.** Cutting managers without redistributing coaching produces
  directionless teams; feedback frequency, not span, is the lever [8].
- **Mandating RTO as a performance intervention.** No performance gain, a measurable hiring and
  retention cost, and researchers characterising some mandates as blame-shifting [14].
- **Running OKRs as a reporting ritual.** Output-shaped key results, frozen quarters and
  unmeasurable KRs are the three documented failure modes [42][43].
- **Letting the board be a formality.** Africa's and India's 2024-25 failures are mostly governance
  failures — no independent director, no minutes, no separation of funds [44][45][47].
- **Treating founder burnout as a private matter.** It propagates: employees report higher anxiety
  and burnout than founders, and most founders never seek help [15][16].
- **Banking AI productivity before measuring it** (95% of pilots showed no P&L impact) [31][32], or
  **blitzscaling a market that is not being decided** — a conditional bet, not a strategy [50][51].
- **Hiring a professional CEO and not actually handing over** — chapter 11's "illusion of turning
  the organization over" is still the most common failure of this transition.

## Key terms
`founder mode` — Graham's (2024) label for a CEO engaging below the org chart, including skip-level
forums, rather than only through direct reports.
`manager mode` — the delegate-through-direct-reports default that founder mode is defined against.
`evidence mode` — Yeh's (2024) guardrail: founder override is legitimate only on evidence and in the
whole company's interest.
`task-relevant maturity` — Grove's rule that management depth varies with the team's experience of
the specific task.
`span of control` — direct reports per manager; average 12.1, median 5-6 (Gallup, 2025).
`delayering / flattening` — deliberately removing management layers to speed decisions and cut cost.
`one-way / two-way door` — irreversible vs reversible decision; sets how much process is justified.
`disagree and commit` — explicit agreement to back a decision you argued against, without consensus.
`DACI / RAPID` — decision-rights notations naming one accountable approver or decider per decision.
`six-pager` — a narrative memo replacing slides, read silently at the start of the meeting.
`operating system (company)` — founding documents + cadence + feedback/performance mechanisms
(Hughes Johnson, 2023).
`eNPS` — employee net promoter score; tech median 17-18 (Culture Amp, 2026).
`agent boss` — Microsoft's (2025) term for an employee who directs and supervises AI agents.
`human-agent ratio` — how many agents per role, and how many humans are needed to supervise them.
`default alive` — Graham's earlier test: would you reach profitability on current growth and spend
without raising again? The post-2022 counterweight to blitzscaling.

## Build ideas for the app
- **Founder mode audit** — headcount, the founder's top 10 recurring decisions, hours by area → a
  per-decision depth recommendation, a skip-level mechanism, and a written override rule [1][4][5].
- **Decision-rights builder** — recurring decisions → one-way/two-way classification, DACI roles,
  deadlines, and a searchable decision log; the cheapest speed increase at 20-200 people [36].
- **Span & layer designer** — org chart, manager IC-time estimates, feedback cadence → layer count
  CEO to junior, managers flagged over 40% IC work or without weekly feedback, and the coaching a
  proposed delayering would orphan [8][25].
- **Goal system chooser** — team size, data availability per metric → an OKR vs North Star vs SMART
  recommendation, outcome-rewritten key results, and a "no data source, no KR" warning list [42][43].
- **Culture pulse + benchmark** — a short quarterly survey → engagement %, eNPS versus the tech
  median (17-18), the two weakest items, and a "what we changed" post to publish [23].
- **Work-model pilot planner** — current policy, roles, commute data → a six-month hybrid or
  four-day pilot design with control group and measures, plus the expected hiring cost of a full RTO
  mandate [10][12][14].
- **Founder wellbeing & continuity check** — sleep, time off, support structures, key-person
  dependencies → a risk score, a 30-day-absence continuity plan, a board disclosure draft [15][21].
- **Board readiness kit** — stage, cap table, jurisdiction → board composition (seed 2:1 → 2:2:1
  with an independent), minute and related-party templates, and the governance questions investors
  in your region will ask [44][47][49].
