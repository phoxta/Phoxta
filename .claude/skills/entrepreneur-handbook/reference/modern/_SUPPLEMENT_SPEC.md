# Supplement spec (for writing reference/modern/*.md files)

Goal: add a **2026 layer** to the distilled HBR Entrepreneur's Handbook (2018). Each supplement
either (a) covers a function the book skips (sales, marketing execution, hiring, operations,
modern metrics, AI-native operating) or (b) refreshes a chapter whose statistics, instruments,
laws or thinking have moved on since 2018 (financing, legal, launch method, leadership, exit,
global lens). Today is September 2026.

## Research rules (non-negotiable)
- **Research with WebSearch and WebFetch.** Aim for 10-20 searches and at least 10 distinct
  sources, published 2024-2026 wherever possible (a classic book or paper may be older; say so).
- **Prefer primary sources:** industry reports (Carta, Bessemer, a16z, Y Combinator, First Round,
  Startup Genome, Crunchbase, PitchBook, CB Insights, ICONIQ, SaaS Capital, Lenny's Newsletter
  surveys, McKinsey, Gartner, Bain, Deloitte), government/regulator sites (SEC, SBA, Companies
  House, HMRC, EU Commission, Startup Genome), the thinkers' own books, essays, talks or
  newsletters, and reputable press (FT, WSJ, Bloomberg, Reuters, TechCrunch, The Information).
- **Every number must be tied to a source you actually fetched or that appeared in a search
  result snippet.** Put the source number in brackets after it, e.g. "median seed pre-money
  $X [3]". If you cannot verify a figure, leave it out or mark it "(unverified)". Never invent a
  URL: only list URLs returned by WebSearch or fetched by WebFetch.
- **Attribute every framework or opinion to its author** with the year and where it was
  published. Distinguish evidence (data) from opinion (a thinker's view).
- **Distill, do not copy.** Quotes <= 25 words, attributed. Write in your own compact words.
- **Global, not US-only.** Where practice differs by region, add a Regional notes section
  (US, UK, EU, Africa - Nigeria especially, India, Southeast Asia, Middle East, LatAm as relevant).
- **AI-era notes are mandatory:** say concretely how AI (assistants, agents, generative tools)
  has changed this function by 2026, with sources, and where the hype outruns the evidence.
- Plain markdown, UTF-8 without BOM, no HTML. Target 200-400 lines.

## Required section order (use exactly these H2 headings)

# S<n> — <Title> (2026 layer)
Supplements: <which handbook chapters / reference files this extends or updates, and why>.

## What changed since 2018
5-10 bullets: the shifts a founder must know (instruments, laws, tools, norms, economics).

## Core ideas (current consensus)
6-12 bullets carrying the modern argument, each attributed.

## Frameworks & playbooks
Each named framework/playbook/model as a list or markdown table: name, author, year, what it
says, when to use it.

## Benchmarks & numbers (2024-2026)
A markdown table: metric | value | segment/context | source [n] | year. Only sourced figures.

## Decision rules & rules of thumb
Concrete if/then guidance and thresholds, one per bullet, each attributed or sourced.

## Process / steps
Numbered procedures a founder can follow.

## Worksheets, checklists & questions
Question lists and checklists that become app screens.

## Regional notes
Bullets per region: what differs (law, funding sources, norms, costs), with sources.

## AI-era notes
How AI changes this function in practice (tools, workflows, risks), evidence vs hype.

## The minds
Table: person | affiliation | key contribution (one line) | where to read (book/essay/newsletter, year).
8-15 entries. Prefer people whose work is current (still publishing 2023-2026).

## Pitfalls
Modern mistakes the sources warn about.

## Key terms
`term` — one-line definition (only terms introduced here).

## Build ideas for the app
4-8 bullets: **Tool name** — inputs → outputs → why a founder needs it.

## Sources
Numbered list: [n] Title — Publisher/Author — date — URL. Only real URLs from your searches/fetches.
