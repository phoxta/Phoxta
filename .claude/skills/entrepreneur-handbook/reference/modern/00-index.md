# The 2026 layer — index

Thirteen researched supplements that sit on top of the distilled HBR Entrepreneur's Handbook
(2018). They do two jobs the book cannot: **cover the functions it skips** (sales, marketing
execution, hiring, operations, modern metrics, AI) and **replace what has gone stale** (financing,
legal, launch method, leadership, exit, and a US-only worldview).

Researched September 2026 from primary sources. Every statistic carries a bracketed source number
resolving to a real URL in that file's Sources list. Roughly 800 cited URLs across the set.

## How to use a supplement

1. Load the handbook chapter first for the method, then the supplement for what is true now.
   The book supplies the questions; the supplement supplies the current answers and numbers.
2. Every file has the same fourteen sections: What changed since 2018, Core ideas, Frameworks and
   playbooks, Benchmarks and numbers, Decision rules, Process, Worksheets, Regional notes,
   AI-era notes, The minds, Pitfalls, Key terms, Build ideas for the app, Sources.
3. **Quote the benchmark with its source number and year.** These numbers move; a 2026 figure
   will be wrong by 2028. Say where it came from and when.
4. **Respect the evidence hierarchy the files mark.** Randomised trials and large primary datasets
   outrank vendor surveys, which outrank a practitioner's opinion. Several files label their own
   weaker citations as secondary or unverified. Carry that label through into your answer.
5. **Regional notes are not optional.** Instrument names, tax reliefs, filing deadlines and
   payment rails differ by country. Ask where the founder is before giving a legal or funding answer.

## Where the handbook and the 2026 layer disagree

These conflicts are real and the app should surface both sides rather than pick silently.

| Question | Handbook (2018) | 2026 layer |
|---|---|---|
| Founder's job as the company grows | Delegate: move from managing content to context, hire professional management (ch 11) | Founder mode says selective depth beats blanket delegation; the reconciliation is depth per task, not per personality (`S11`) |
| The plan | A ~40-page business plan is the artefact (ch 5) | A one-pager, deck, memo and data room; the long plan survives for banks, grants and visas (`S13`) |
| Product-market fit | Evaluate the opportunity against five characteristics, once (ch 2) | PMF has levels, is measured continuously, and can be lost (`S10`) |
| Who to back | Trait clusters and personal background (ch 1) | Prior same-industry experience predicts success; traits do not (`S13`) |
| Legal form | Choose for where you are going; C corp for a sale or IPO (ch 4) | Same logic, but QSBS after July 2025 makes the timing of C-corp stock issuance financially material (`S09`) |
| Valuation | Methods described, no multiples given (appendix C) | Actual 2026 multiples and discount rates, plus SDE vs EBITDA for small businesses (`S12`) |
| Scale | Grow when the strategy is sustainable (ch 10) | Blitzscaling was re-priced after 2022; efficiency ratios now gate growth (`S05`, `S11`) |
