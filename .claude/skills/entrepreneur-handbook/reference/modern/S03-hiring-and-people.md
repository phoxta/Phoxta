# S03 — Hiring and people: co-founders to the first 50 (2026 layer)
Supplements: the book has no hiring or people-operations chapter. This file extends Stage 0
(`01-founder-fit.md`: "bring in a co-owner who has it" — but not how to choose one or split equity),
Stage 3 (`04-legal-structure.md`: partnership agreements → founder vesting), Stage 6 (the book gap:
first employees, contractors, pay, onboarding) and Stage 8 (`10-sustaining-growth.md` "hiring to
scale", `11-leadership.md` delegation, `12-entrepreneurial-spirit.md` culture). Chapters 10-12 cover
what the founder must become; this file covers who to hire, how, for how much, and where.

## What changed since 2018
- **Co-founder splits are converging on equal, and solo founding is common but funded less.** Two-founder
  teams splitting 50/50 rose from 31.5% (2015) to 45.9% (2024); solo founders were 35% of new startups
  in 2024 but only 17% of those that closed a VC round [1][2].
- **Equity became a benchmarked market.** Carta (45,000+ cap tables), Pave (8,000+ companies) and Index
  Ventures' OptionPlan (20,000+ grants, 1,650+ startups) replaced folklore with medians [3][10][61].
  Four-year/one-year-cliff is still the default, but private-company ongoing grants now vest in ~3.5
  years and refresh grants are performance-gated, not automatic [28].
- **Selection science was revised.** Sackett et al. (2022) re-ran the classic meta-analyses with better
  corrections: structured interviews (r = .42) now top the table and cognitive ability fell to .31 [13].
- **Hiring is global by default.** An employer-of-record (EOR) turns a compliant hire in 150+ countries
  into a ~$499-599/month subscription [37]; Deel processed 1M+ worker contracts across 37,000+ companies in
  2025 [35]. Classification enforcement tightened in parallel (US DOL economic-reality rule, California
  ABC test, UK IR35 thresholds, Nigerian courts on substance-over-form) [32][33][34].
- **Remote settled into structured hybrid, then met a 2025-26 return-to-office push.** 66% of US firms
  still offer location flexibility (Flex Index Q3 2025), but required office days rose ~12% since early
  2024 and fully on-site job postings jumped from 65% (Q4 2025) to 87% (Q2 2026) in Robert Half's
  series [40][41].
- **AI sits on both sides of the table.** 39% of organisations used AI in HR by Dec 2025 (26% in 2024)
  [51]; ~40% of candidates use AI to apply and 6% admit to interview fraud [48]. Regulators answered:
  NYC Local Law 144 (bias audits, 2023) and the EU AI Act, which classes recruitment AI as high-risk from
  2 Aug 2026 [43][47].
- **Startups run leaner.** Average Series A headcount fell from ~21 to ~15.9 [3]; startup hiring slowed in
  every sector but hardware in H1 2025 while salaries rose and equity stayed flat [4].
- **UK EMI expanded (6 Apr 2026): company cap £3m → £6m, gross assets £30m → £120m, headcount 250 →
  500** [29]; India's DPIIT startups defer ESOP tax to exit; SEBI (Jun 2025) let founders keep ESOPs
  through IPO [59][60].
- **Performance management moved to a quarterly rhythm** — 39% of high-performing HR teams review
  quarterly vs 10% of low performers [54].

## Core ideas (current consensus)
- **Scorecard before search.** Define the mission, 3-8 measurable outcomes and the competencies of the
  role, then hunt for an "A player": someone with a 90% chance of achieving outcomes only the top 10% of
  candidates could (Smart & Street, *Who*, 2008) [17]. Mochary (2020) makes it a 90-day roadmap [18].
- **Structure beats intuition.** Structured interviews are the single most valid tool (r = .42), ahead of
  job-knowledge tests (.40), biodata (.38), work samples (.33) and cognitive ability (.31) — evidence, not
  opinion (Sackett et al. 2022) [13]. Google's data: four interviews predict with 86% confidence; each
  extra interviewer adds ~1% (Bock, *Work Rules!*, 2015) [16].
- **References are the real interview.** Run 6-8 (Altman) to 10-20 (Zappacosta) calls, half of them
  backchannel, and ask "who else should I talk to that might tell me this could be a mistake?" (First
  Round Review, 2024) [22]. Mochary: check references you choose, not the candidate's list [18].
- **Hire for the next 12-18 months, not forever.** Shorter and they can't scale; longer and you over-hire
  a bored executive (Gil, *High Growth Handbook*, 2018) [19].
- **Hiring is a network effect.** "The first 100 hires define the next 200"; the best predictor of a
  hire's quality is who referred them (Graham, First Round, 2015) [20]. Mochary's sourcing loop: ask every
  strong person "who are the three most talented people you know?" [18].
- **Founder-led sales first, engineers first.** Across 20 top B2B startups, >2/3 hired an engineer first,
  100% had one in the first three, and sales became the second most common role only inside the first ten
  (Rachitsky, 2023) [23].
- **Split founder equity near-equally, vest everyone, never on performance metrics.** "All the work is
  ahead of you" (Y Combinator / Seibel); vesting and a cliff apply to founders too [11][12]. Carta data
  shows teams drifting this way [1].
- **Pay is a system, not a negotiation.** Benchmark salary and equity by role, level and location; tie
  refresh grants to rating (Pave 2025: ~100% of promoted staff refreshed vs ~20-30% of "meets") [28].
- **Adequate performance gets a generous severance** — the keeper test (McCord/Netflix, HBR 2014;
  *Powerful*, 2018). Opinion, contested, and culturally loaded outside the US [25][26].
- **Give away your Legos.** Leaders who grow with the company hand their job away every few months;
  30-50 people is where "a family becomes a company" and things must be written down (Graham) [20].
- **Onboarding is the manager's job.** New hires are 3.4x more likely to call onboarding exceptional when
  the manager is involved (Gallup, via [55]); 86% decide how long they'll stay within six months [55].
- **Write the operating system down.** Founding documents, hiring plans, role definitions, feedback and
  performance mechanisms as one toolkit with templates (Hughes Johnson, *Scaling People*, 2023) [27].

## Decision rules & rules of thumb
- **If you cannot write the scorecard, you are not ready to hire.** Mission, outcomes, competencies first;
  job descriptions are marketing, scorecards are the contract (Smart & Street) [17].
- **Split founder equity equally unless one founder has already de-risked the business** (product, revenue,
  capital); then adjust by a few points, never by "idea credit". Vest all founders 4 years / 1-year cliff
  (YC) [11][12]; the data trend supports it [1].
- **Solo founders: expect a funding penalty** (35% of startups, 17% of VC rounds) and recruit a co-founder or
  a senior first hire with meaningful equity [2].
- **Hire #1 is almost always an engineer unless the founders are technical; hire sales only once founder-led
  sales has closed repeatable deals** (Rachitsky) [23]. Hire a recruiter by ~hire 8-10 if you plan 20+ in a
  year — it recurs across top startups [23].
- **Four structured interviews, then stop.** Each extra interviewer buys ~1% predictive power at a large
  candidate-experience cost (Bock) [16]. Replace one interview with a paid work sample when the role
  produces artefacts [13].
- **Never extend an offer without references you chose.** If a candidate will not enable backchannel calls
  before the offer, delay the offer (First Round; Mochary) [22][18].
- **Exec hire size test:** would this person be the right leader for a company 2-3x today's size in 18
  months? If they are right for 5 years out, they are too senior (Gil) [19].
- **Pool sizing:** create ~10% at seed and top up to 12-15% at Series A rather than over-reserving early —
  every unused option is founder dilution (Index) [8]. In APAC/ME, 11% at seed is the median [6].
- **First ten grants total ~5% (US) or 3-4% (Europe); a senior engineer ≈ 1%, mid ≈ 0.45%, junior ≈
  0.15%** (Index) [7]. Below-market cash → above-benchmark equity, and say so in the offer [9].
- **Refreshes are earned:** budget them for promotions and "above" ratings; a "meets" refresh is the
  exception (Pave) [28].
- **Contractor vs employee: if you control how, when and where the work is done, they work only for you,
  and the work is core to the business, they are an employee in the US, UK and Nigeria alike**
  [32][33][34]. Use an EOR ($499-599/month) before that line is crossed abroad [37].
- **Remote policy: pick structured hybrid (3 days) or fully remote with in-person weeks — mandates without
  attendance enforcement raise required days 12% and actual days 1-3%** (Flex Index) [40].
- **Onboarding: the manager runs day 1 to day 90.** Ship the 90-day roadmap and a buddy before start;
  manager involvement is worth 3.4x on perceived quality [18][55].
- **Performance: quarterly written check-ins, continuous feedback, a documented 7/30/60/90 PIP before any
  termination** (Mochary; Lattice) [18][54].
- **AI in hiring: use it to draft, schedule, transcribe and summarise; never to reject.** Keep a trained
  human with override authority on every decision, tell candidates, and log it — required in the EU from
  Aug 2026 and in NYC now [43][47].

## Process / steps
1. **Choose and paper the co-founder(s).** Test the relationship with a 4-8 week build sprint; agree roles,
   decision rights, time commitment and exit terms; split near-equally; sign vesting (4/1) and IP
   assignment at incorporation; pick the pool size for the first round [11][12][8].
2. **Plan the first ten.** Map the next 12-18 months of outcomes → roles in sequence (engineering → design
   /CS → sales/PM → recruiter); decide location and legal route per role (local employee, EOR, contractor)
   [23][19][37].
3. **Write the scorecard** (mission, 3-8 outcomes, competencies) and the 90-day roadmap; convert the
   scorecard into a structured interview kit with rubrics and one work sample [17][18][16].
4. **Source.** Ask every strong contact for their three most talented people; cold outreach on LinkedIn/
   GitHub; post to startup boards; pay referral bonuses; publish a one-page "how we work" doc as the
   employer brand [18][23][27].
5. **Select.** Screening call (30 min) → "Who" chronological interview → focused interviews (4 total,
   independent scores) → work sample → decision meeting against the scorecard [17][16].
6. **Reference.** 6-8 calls minimum, half backchannel; ask the comparison, 1-100, criticism and "why would
   they leave in three months" questions; kill the offer on a pattern, not one voice [22].
7. **Offer.** Benchmark base (Carta/Pave), set equity from the level table, state strike price, vesting,
   cliff, exercise window and refresh policy in plain language; UK: grant under EMI where eligible [7][28][29].
8. **Contract and comply.** Employee vs contractor test per country; EOR or local entity; PAYE/pension/
   NSITF-type registrations; keep a written classification memo [32][33][34].
9. **Onboard (day −7 to day 90).** Roadmap and buddy before day 1; laptop and access on day 1; manager
   30/60/90 check-ins; explicit "you are on track / not" at day 90 [18][55].
10. **Run performance.** Written quarterly check-ins, continuous feedback, comp review once a year with a
    refresh matrix, PIP ladder with documentation, generous severance when it is not working [54][28][18][25].
11. **Re-plan at every doubling** (10 → 20 → 50): write down mission, values and decision rights at 30-50;
    add a recruiter and a people lead; give away your Legos [20].

## Worksheets, checklists & questions
**Co-founder compatibility (answer separately, then compare)**
- What does each of us own, and who decides when we disagree?
- Hours per week for the next 24 months; other commitments; runway each can survive without salary?
- What is each founder's number for "we should stop"? For "we should sell"?
- What did each of us de-risk before today (code, customers, cash)? Does that justify an unequal split?
- Vesting 4 years, 1-year cliff, acceleration on change of control — agreed for all founders? [11][12]

**Scorecard template (one page)** — Mission (one sentence) · Outcomes (3-8, measurable, dated) ·
Competencies (5-8 behaviours, incl. culture) · 90-day roadmap · Who interviews for what [17][18].

**Interview-loop checklist** — 4 interviewers max, each owns distinct outcomes · fixed questions and a
1-4 rubric written before the loop · scores submitted before the debrief · one work sample, paid ·
decision meeting ends in yes/no, not "maybe" [16][17].

**Reference-call script (eight questions)** — best you've seen in this role? · 1-100 and why not 100? ·
a time you gave critical feedback? · how do they handle disagreement? · will they thrive in *this* role?
· what did you see them build? · fit with my management style? · if they left in 3 months, why? · who
else should I talk to? [22]

**Offer checklist** — base vs benchmark · equity % and share count · strike price and 409A/valuation
basis · vesting, cliff, acceleration · post-termination exercise window · refresh policy · location tier
· EMI/DPIIT status where relevant · start date and 90-day roadmap attached [7][28][29][59].

**Contractor-or-employee test (any country)** — Do we control how/when/where? · Can they send a
substitute? · Do they work mainly for us? · Is the work core to the business? · Do we supply the tools?
· Is there ongoing mutual obligation? Three or more "yes" → treat as employee or use an EOR [32][33][34].

**AI-in-hiring compliance checklist** — inventory every tool that ranks, filters or scores people ·
is any output "heavily influencing" who advances? (then high-risk in the EU) · human with authority and
training reviews every reject · candidates notified before use; NYC: opt-out notice + annual bias audit
published · logs kept ≥ 6 months · no emotion recognition · vendor's technical documentation on file
[43][45][46][47].

**First-90-days checklist** — roadmap shared pre-start · buddy assigned (15 min/day, two weeks) · week-1
customer exposure · day-30 written check-in · day-60 first outcome shipped · day-90 explicit verdict [18].

## Regional notes
- **US:** at-will employment; options need a 409A valuation; ISOs vs NSOs; DOL six-factor economic-reality
  test and California's ABC test with $25k-100k per-contractor fines for misclassification [33]; NYC LL144
  bias audits for any automated screening tool used on NYC candidates [47]; US seed hires expect options
  immediately (Index) [9]; salaries and equity benchmarks: Carta, Pave [4][28].
- **UK:** EMI is the default early-stage scheme — £250k per employee, £6m company cap, ≤500 staff and
  £120m gross assets from 6 Apr 2026, no income tax/NIC at exercise if strike ≥ market value, BADR CGT 18%
  after a 24-month hold, ≥25 hrs/week or 75% working-time eligibility [29][30][31]. IR35: small companies
  (turnover ≤ £15m, balance sheet ≤ £7.5m, ≤ 50 staff, from 6 Apr 2025) leave status to the contractor;
  above that you issue a Status Determination Statement; penalties 30-100% of lost tax with 4/6/20-year
  lookbacks [32]. Personio is the common UK/EU SME HRIS [39]. Check local law on the Employment Rights
  Act 2025 changes (unverified here).
- **EU:** high-risk AI obligations for recruitment, promotion, allocation and monitoring apply from 2 Aug
  2026 (Digital Omnibus may defer to no later than Dec 2027/Aug 2028 — plan as if it holds) [42][43];
  workplace emotion recognition banned and AI-literacy duty live since 2 Feb 2025 [45]; inform and consult
  workers' representatives before deployment [42]; Commission draft guidelines (30 Jun 2026) put CV
  screening, ranking and background checks in scope and employer-branding tools out [46]. European employees
  own roughly half what US employees do (~10% vs ~20% late stage) and options skew to executives — a
  recruiting lever if you break the pattern [7][8].
- **Africa / Nigeria:** courts apply control, integration, economic-reality, mutuality and exclusivity
  tests; misclassification exposes you to Labour Act entitlements, National Industrial Court claims,
  permanent-establishment tax and Immigration Act sanctions [34]. Lagos back-end developers earn ₦1.2-2.5m
  per month (~$800-1,667), CTOs ₦3-5m+; naira weakness pushes senior talent to dollar-paying remote roles,
  tightening local supply [56][57]. EORs with Nigerian coverage: Deel, Remote, Multiplier, Bento Africa,
  Native Teams [58]; Deel's top-funded startups already source from Kenya and Nigeria for AI-training roles
  [35]. Pay in USD/stablecoin is a retention lever across volatile currencies (84.6% of Argentine Deel
  workers chose USD) [35]. Check local law: PAYE, pension (PenCom) and NSITF registrations (unverified here).
- **India:** ESOP pools 10-15% early, 4-year/1-year cliff; DPIIT-recognised startups defer perquisite tax
  to sale/exit; SEBI (Jun 2025) lets founders keep ESOPs granted ≥1 year before the DRHP through IPO
  [59][60]. Carta's APAC/ME data: pools 7% pre-seed → 11% seed [6].
- **Southeast Asia / Middle East:** smaller pools than the US (11-13% at later stages vs 18%) and more
  conservative hiring in 2025 [6]; expect cash-heavier offers and EOR-first hiring across jurisdictions.
- **LatAm:** cross-border contractor hiring is common; currency risk drives USD/stablecoin pay preferences
  (Deel) [35]; classification rules vary by country — use an EOR or local counsel.

## AI-era notes
- **What is real:** AI now drafts scorecards, job posts and interview kits, transcribes and summarises
  interviews, schedules, and writes first-draft reviews — 48% of HR teams use it to write performance reviews
  and 45% to summarise feedback [54]; 93% of recruiters plan to increase AI use in 2026 [51]. For a founder
  this collapses the admin cost of *structured* hiring, which was the main reason startups skipped it.
- **What the evidence does not support:** claims that AI screening removes bias. Only 26% of candidates trust
  AI to evaluate them fairly [49]; 66% of Americans say they would not apply where AI helps decide (Pew, via
  [51]); 21% of AI-using US companies let AI reject at every stage with no human review [52] — exactly the
  practice regulators target. No fetched source shows AI screening out-predicts a structured interview.
- **The application flood:** ~40% of candidates use AI to write applications [48], 76.9% of recruiters report
  encountering AI-generated applications (Jan 2026, up from 53% in H1 2024) [52], 6% admit interview fraud and
  Gartner forecasts 1 in 4 profiles fake by 2028 [48]. Counter-moves: work samples, live problem-solving, in-
  person or camera-on final rounds (62% of candidates prefer employers who interview in person) [48], identity
  checks for remote hires, and a small paid trial before the offer.
- **Law:** EU AI Act — recruitment, selection, promotion, allocation and monitoring AI is high-risk; deployer
  duties are trained human oversight with override authority, worker/candidate notice, consultation, ≥6-month
  logs, DPIA; fines up to €35m/7% (prohibited practices) and €15m/3% (high-risk breaches); applies to non-EU
  companies recruiting EU candidates [42][43][44][45]. NYC LL144 — annual independent bias audit, published
  summary, candidate notice with opt-out, up to $1,500 per violation per day; a Dec 2025 Comptroller audit found
  enforcement "ineffective" (75% of 311 complaints misrouted; 17 potential non-compliance issues in 32 audits
  the regulator had cleared) — expect tightening, not relief [47].
- **New roles:** "AI trainer" was the fastest-growing cross-border role on Deel in 2025 (+283%, 70,000+
  workers, $15-75/hour) [35][36]; Carta reports AI roles among the fastest-rising startup salaries [4].
- **Agentic recruiting** (autonomous sourcing/outreach/screening agents) is the 2026 vendor pitch; treat any
  agent that ranks or rejects as a high-risk system under the EU test ("output heavily influences who
  advances") [46], and keep the human decision meeting.

## Pitfalls
- Splitting founder equity by "who had the idea" or on performance metrics; skipping founder vesting [11][12].
- Hiring a salesperson before founder-led sales works, or an executive sized for five years out [23][19].
- Unstructured "culture fit" chats with six interviewers — lower validity, higher cost [13][16].
- Treating references as a formality or calling only the candidate's list [22][18].
- Reserving a 20% pool at seed and letting it dilute the founders while unused (Index) [8].
- Silent geo discounts on equity that candidates discover later (Pave: 29-36%) [28].
- Contractors who are employees in substance — back tax, penalties and, in Nigeria, immigration exposure
  [32][33][34].
- RTO mandates without a reason or enforcement: required days up, attendance flat, trust down [40].
- Letting an AI tool reject candidates unreviewed; deploying screening AI on EU or NYC candidates without
  notice, audit and oversight [43][47][52].
- No 90-day roadmap: 22% of hires leave within 90 days and a failed hire costs $25-50k [55].
- Importing the keeper test into a 15-person team as a policy rather than a leadership question [26].

## Key terms
`scorecard` — one-page definition of a role's mission, measurable outcomes and competencies (Smart & Street).
`A player` — candidate with a ≥90% chance of achieving outcomes only the top 10% could (Smart & Street).
`structured interview` — same questions, same rubric, independent scoring; validity r = .42 (Sackett 2022).
`work sample` — a paid, realistic task used as a predictor (r = .33).
`backchannel reference` — a reference the hiring team finds, not one the candidate supplies.
`option pool / ESOP` — shares reserved for employees, sized as % of fully diluted equity by stage.
`cliff` — the period (usually 12 months) before any equity vests.
`refresh grant` — additional equity to an existing employee, now typically rating-gated.
`EMI` — UK Enterprise Management Incentives, tax-advantaged options for companies ≤500 staff (from Apr 2026).
`BADR` — UK Business Asset Disposal Relief; 18% CGT on qualifying EMI gains from 6 Apr 2026.
`IR35 / SDS` — UK off-payroll rules; a Status Determination Statement records the client's decision.
`EOR` — employer of record: a third party legally employs your worker abroad for a monthly fee.
`AEDT` — automated employment decision tool (NYC Local Law 144 term).
`high-risk AI system` — EU AI Act class covering recruitment, selection, promotion, allocation, monitoring.
`keeper test` — Netflix question: would I fight to keep this person? If not, exit them generously.
`tour of duty` — a defined 2-4 year mission with mutual promises (Hoffman, Casnocha, Yeh).
`structured hybrid` — fixed in-office days per week set by the employer (Flex Index term).
