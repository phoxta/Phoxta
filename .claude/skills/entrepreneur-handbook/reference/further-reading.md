# Further Reading

Source: HBR's Entrepreneur's Handbook (2018), pp. 273-276. Grouped as in the book, by part and then articles vs. books. HBR product numbers are kept in parentheses.

## Part 1: Preparing for the Journey

### Articles
- Andreessen, Marc, and Adi Ignatius. "In Search of the Next Big Thing." Harvard Business Review, May 2013 (#R1305G). — Andreessen Horowitz cofounder on today's entrepreneurship challenges.
- Bhidé, Amar. "The Questions Every Entrepreneur Must Answer." Harvard Business Review, November–December 1996 (#96603). — Classic: pause the bias for action; check personal goals and strategy.
- Butler, Timothy. "Hiring an Entrepreneurial Leader." Harvard Business Review, March–April 2017 (#1702E). — HBS research on what makes successful entrepreneurial leaders.
- Valencia, Jordana. "How Founders Can Recognize and Combat Depression." HBR.org, February 17, 2017. — Entrepreneurs are 30 percent likelier to experience depression; addressing and preventing it.

### Books
- Ruback, Richard S., and Royce Yudkoff. HBR Guide to Buying a Small Business (HBR Guide Series). Harvard Business Review Press, 2016. — Run your own company by buying one instead of starting from scratch.

## Part 2: Defining Your Enterprise

### Articles
- Blank, Steve. "Why the Lean Start-Up Changes Everything." Harvard Business Review, May 2013 (#1305C). — Introduces an experimental approach to creating a new business.
- Brown, Tim. "Design Thinking." Harvard Business Review, June 2008 (#R0806E). — Giving innovation a human-centred approach.
- Hagiu, Andrei, and Simon Rothman. "Network Effects Aren't Enough." Harvard Business Review, April 2016 (#R1604D). — Avoiding the pitfalls of fast-growing platform businesses.
- Kavadias, Stelios, et al. "The Transformative Business Model." Harvard Business Review, October 2016 (#R1610H). — How an innovative business model changes an industry and builds a business.
- Ladd, Ted. "The Limits of the Lean Start-Up Method." HBR.org, March 7, 2016. — Lean works, but there are other things to keep in mind.
- Ovans, Andrea. "What Is a Business Model?" HBR.org, January 23, 2015. — Primer on business models and how the concept evolved over two decades.
- Magretta, Joan. "Why Business Models Matter." Harvard Business Review, May 2002 (#R0205F). — What a business model is, how it differs from strategy, why it matters.
- McGrath, Rita Gunther. "Transient Advantage." Harvard Business Review, June 2013 (#R1306C). — Sustainable advantage is no longer viable; how smart companies stay ahead.
- Sahlman, William A. "How to Write a Great Business Plan." Harvard Business Review, July–August 1997 (#97409). — Classic: financiers care about people, opportunity, context, and risk-versus-reward, not pages of numbers.
- Thomke, Stefan, and Donald Reinertsen. "Six Myths of Product Development." Harvard Business Review, May 2012 (#R1205E). — Product development differs from manufacturing and needs new management.
- Van Alstyne, Marshall W., et al. "Pipelines, Platforms, and the New Rules of Strategy." Harvard Business Review, April 2016 (#R1604C). — How platforms achieve impressive growth and how their structure rewrites strategy.

### Books
- Harvard Business Review. Creating Business Plans (HBR 20-Minute Manager Series). Harvard Business Review Press, 2014. — Fundamentals of crafting a business plan.
- Osterwalder, Alexander. Business Model Generation: A Handbook for Visionaries, Game Changers, and Challengers. Wiley, 2010. — Guide to experimenting with and iterating on business models.
- Ries, Eric. The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use Continuous Innovation to Create Radically Successful Businesses. Crown, 2011. — The book that first introduced lean entrepreneurship in detail.
- Sheen, Raymond, with Amy Gallo. HBR Guide to Building Your Business Case (HBR Guides Series). Harvard Business Review Press, 2015. — Crafting an appealing business case document, for startups and corporate innovators alike.

## Part 3: Financing Your Business

### Articles
- Anderson, Chris. "How to Give a Killer Presentation." Harvard Business Review, June 2013 (#R1306K). — TED curator's primer on hooking your audience.
- Mulcahy, Diane. "Six Myths About Venture Capitalists." Harvard Business Review, May 2013 (#R1305E). — Clear-eyed view of the VC ecosystem for entrepreneurs.
- Mullins, John. "Use Customer Cash to Finance Your Start-Up." Harvard Business Review, July–August 2013 (#F1307A). — Scalable tech startups fund early growth from customers, avoiding outside capital.
- Zider, Bob, and Hal R. Varian. "How Venture Capital Works." Harvard Business Review, November–December 1998 (#98611). — Classic on the model that drives venture capitalists.

### Books
- Baehr, Evan, and Evan Loomis. Get Backed: Craft Your Story, Build the Perfect Pitch Deck, and Launch the Venture of Your Dreams. Harvard Business Review Press, 2016. — Handbook for writing a pitch deck and presenting it to funders.
- Berinato, Scott. Good Charts: The HBR Guide to Making Smarter, More Persuasive Data Visualizations. Harvard Business Review Press, 2016. — Persuasive data visualisations for your business plan or pitch deck.
- Bussgang, Jeffrey. Mastering the VC Game: A Venture Capital Insider Reveals How to Get from Start-Up to IPO on Your Terms. Portfolio, 2011. — Understand the VC ecosystem to pick the right partner.
- Duarte, Nancy. HBR Guide to Persuasive Presentations (HBR Guides Series). Harvard Business Review Press, 2012. — High-stakes pitches: a simple compelling story deck plus authentic speaking style.
- Harvard Business Review. HBR Guide to Finance Basics for Managers (HBR Guides Series). Harvard Business Review Press, 2012. — What you need to know about the numbers.

## Part 4: Scaling Up

### Articles
- Bower, Joseph L., and Clayton M. Christensen. "Disruptive Technologies: Catching the Wave." Harvard Business Review, January–February 1995 (#95103). — The seminal article on disruptive innovation.
- Christensen, Clayton M., and Michael Overdorf. "Meeting the Challenge of Disruptive Change." Harvard Business Review, March 2000 (#R00202). — How established organisations stay innovative and avoid disruption by new entrants.
- Churchill, Neil C., and Virginia L. Lewis. "Five Stages of Small Business Growth." Harvard Business Review, May 1983 (#83301). — Classic path from startup to established business and the problems at each stage.
- Govindarajan, Vijay. "Great Innovators Create the Future, Manage the Present, and Selectively Forget the Past." HBR.org, March 31, 2016. — Beyond ambidexterity: also shed the values and beliefs tying you to the past.
- Hoffman, Reid, and Tim Sullivan. "Blitzscaling." Harvard Business Review, April 2016 (#R1604B). — Managing the spectacularly rapid growth of some startup wunderkinds.
- Zook, Chris, and James Allen. "Reigniting Growth." Harvard Business Review, March 2016 (#R1603F). — Using the "founder's mentality" to keep growing as an established company.

### Books
- Christensen, Clayton M. The Innovator's Dilemma, 2nd ed. Harvard Business Review Press, 2013. — Fuller treatment of Christensen's theory of disruptive innovation.

## Part 5: Looking to the Future

### Articles
- Wasserman, Noam. "The Founder's Dilemma." Harvard Business Review, February 2008 (#R0802G). — Classic question for founders: do you want to be rich, or king?
