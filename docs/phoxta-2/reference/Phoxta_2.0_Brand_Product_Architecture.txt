1. Strategic Foundation and Category Definition


Phoxta should own the space between inspiration and venture creation.
The category is not simply 'startup education', 'business idea generator', 'AI
business builder', or 'market research'. It is the integrated workflow
connecting all four.

1.1 The problem Phoxta exists to solve

Starting a business is often presented as a building problem: create a website, write a plan, make a
logo, launch ads. In practice, the earlier problem is deciding what deserves to be built. People can
spend months executing an idea whose customer, timing, economics or differentiation were never
understood.

                                       Phoxta 2.0 Brand & Product Architecture

--- PAGE 5 ---
 Aspiring founders struggle to identify credible opportunities rather than generic startup ideas.
 Market research is fragmented across search engines, reports, social platforms, spreadsheets
  and AI chats.
 Validation is frequently reduced to superficial scoring rather than explicit assumptions and
   evidence.
 Education is detached from execution: people learn concepts but still have to assemble a
   separate operating stack.
 Pre-built business offers often hide the underlying opportunity thesis, making them feel like
   templates rather than ventures.

1.2 Category statement

 CATEGORY

 Opportunity Discovery & Venture Creation Platform


This category gives Phoxta permission to serve users before they have an idea, during research and
validation, while building, and after launch. It also accommodates Ready-to-Launch Businesses
without making the company sound like a marketplace of generic turnkey websites.

1.3 Strategic thesis

 THESIS

 The world is not short of ideas. The scarce resource is clarity about which problems, shifts
 and unmet needs can become valuable businesses - and a practical system for acting on that
  clarity.

5.5 Signal taxonomy

 Pain signals: complaints, manual work, delays, errors, high service costs, repeated workarounds.
 Demand signals: active search, procurement, job roles, budget allocation, growing category
   spend.
 Technology signals: newly feasible automation, falling inference costs, multimodal capabilities,
   APIs and infrastructure.
 Regulatory signals: new obligations, reporting requirements, compliance burdens or
   deregulation.
 Behaviour signals: changing habits, new channels, new expectations, demographic or workforce
    shifts.
 Supply signals: fragmented vendors, poor service, low digitisation, weak customer experience.
 Business-model signals: new pricing models, unbundling/rebundling, verticalisation, embedded
   services.
 Distribution signals: new communities, platforms, marketplaces, ecosystems or partnerships.

6.4 Decision review

Phoxta should avoid declaring ideas 'good' or 'bad'. The decision review should show:

 What has been learned.
 Which assumptions now have evidence.
 Which assumptions remain uncertain.
 What changed from the original thesis.
 What the next cheapest learning action is.
 Four user-controlled decisions: Proceed, Revise, Pause, Stop.

 CRM, pipeline and customer-support setup.
 Analytics events and feedback collection.
 Task plan with dependencies, owners and milestones.

7.4 Launch

 Launch objective and target cohort.
 Channel plan: founder-led sales, partnerships, communities, content, outbound, paid acquisition
  where appropriate.
 Messaging and campaign assets.
 Sales scripts and objection handling.
 Onboarding and activation checklist.
 Measurement dashboard: acquisition, activation, conversion, retention, revenue and learning.
 Post-launch learning loop back into Opportunity Brief.

15. Trust, Evidence, AI Transparency and Quality
Controls



 Every externally sourced fact should retain source, date and geography.
 AI-generated statements should be labelled as hypotheses or synthesis, not evidence.
 Opportunity pages should show 'What we know / What we infer / What we still need to test'.
 Market-size estimates should expose assumptions and methodology.
 Generated competitors must be verified before being presented as factual entities.
 Ready-to-Launch listings must disclose dependencies, ongoing software/API costs and what has
   or has not been market-tested.
 Avoid success probabilities and guaranteed-income language.
 Maintain version history so users can see how an opportunity thesis changed as evidence
   arrived.
 Allow users to challenge or correct AI outputs and attach their own evidence.
